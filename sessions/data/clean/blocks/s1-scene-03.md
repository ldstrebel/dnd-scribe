## CHAPTER 3: FROST AND BONE

The shockwave of Eusacles's strike still rang through the high rotunda, a cloud of white marble dust settling over the scarred flagstones. <!-- L1017 -->

Deprived of an easy kill, the ink blob flattened its gelatinous mass and squelched across the tiles, slithering with unnatural speed directly toward the opposite side of the colonnade. <!-- L1018 -->

Professor Edward Dravin froze. The living puddle of viscous tar was bearing down on his polished leather dress shoes. Instinct took over before academic reason could catch up. The professor recoiled, staggering backward a half-step and thrusting both open palms forward in pure, startled revulsion—the universal reflex of a man trying to fend off a feral dog. <!-- L1019 -->

He didn't chant pseudo-Latin incantations. He didn't invoke mythological gods. He merely shoved his hands out in distaste.

Yet the moment his fingers locked rigid in the air, the temperature inside the library plummeted to sub-zero. A bitter, stinging frost crept across his tweed cuffs, numbing his fingertips. Beneath the encroaching blob, the polished marble fractured with an eerie, pale radiance. <!-- L1020 -->

With a sickening crack of splintering stone, a massive skeletal hand forged of yellowed bone and cemetery frost burst directly through the flagstones. Its calcified fingers snapped shut around the ink creature, squeezing the tar with crushing force and dragging it violently downward into the stone seams. The blob let out a wet, screeching hiss as necrotic cold burned through its viscous body. <!-- L1021 -->

Dravin stumbled back against a bookshelf, his breath frosting in the sudden chill. He stared at his trembling, open palms in sheer disbelief, his wire-rimmed spectacles slipping down his sweat-slick nose. He was a history professor. He lectured on Peloponnesian pottery and classical rhetoric. He did not cause dead things to claw out of floors. <!-- L1093 -->

Across the rotunda, the larger horned fiend bellowed in rage. The creature bared its needle-thin fangs, dark ink boiling along its shoulders as it witnessed its minion trapped in bone. <!-- L1094 -->

Lowering its heavy, ridged brow, the beast dug its cloven hooves into the marble and charged across the rotunda like a freight train. Eusacles braced himself, but the monster was already upon him. <!-- L1095 -->

The horned fiend slammed full-tilt into Eusacles’s chest. The violent impact cracked like thunder, lifting the broad-shouldered gambler off his feet and throwing him hard against the fluted base of a Doric column. The shimmering mantle of light surrounding his torso flared brilliantly under the blow, absorbing the crushing kinetic force before flickering out. <!-- L1096 -->

Near the perimeter stacks, Pierre dropped flat behind an overturned pedestal, clutching his canvas rucksack over his head like a bomb shelter. His knuckles turned white against the fabric, his woolen beret pulled down so hard it bent the rims of his glasses. If he kept quiet, if he didn't move, if he just pretended he was back in a Paris café reading monster lore, maybe the horned demon would ignore him completely. <!-- L1104 -->

At the base of the pillar, Eusacles coughed, spitting a fleck of blood onto the marble as he rolled his shoulders and shoved himself upright. "Man, that hurt... but you hit like a punk." <!-- L1109 -->

Between the fallen column and the struggling ink blob, the tiny bundle woven by the Fates stirred. <!-- L1110 -->

The little figure crafted from weathered driftwood and threadbare sailcloth pushed itself up from the cold stone. With a quiet, creaking rustle of dry timber, it raised its right arm, testing the balance of its gleaming sewing-needle rapier. Its carved wooden jaw tilted upward, button eyes of polished shell gleaming beneath its mop of green kelp. <!-- L1115 -->

Behind the loom, the eldest Fate pointed a gnarled finger across the floor, her voice shrill over the clatter of the loom. "Hold them off! Do not let them tear the veil!" <!-- L1118 -->

The horned fiend hissed, pawing the stone as the wounded ink blob thrashed against the skeletal grip, readying for another clash. <!-- L1119 -->
