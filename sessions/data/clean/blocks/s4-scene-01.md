## CHAPTER 26: THE MEDUSA PROTOCOL

In the high-ceilinged atrium of the North Carolina Museum of World and North Carolina History, the scent of floor wax and climate-controlled coolness hung still in the air. Beyond the velvet stanchions, the celebrated ancient limestone tablet rested under its heavy vitrine, the fractured upper edge awaiting the missing shard. <!-- L0105 -->

Behind the display, Pierre and Alfie wheeled out the metal archaeology cart. On its upper tray lay the artificial limestone fragment they had shaped and dusted in the back rooms, imbued with Alfie's razor-sharp wordcraft to mimic the missing ancient script. <!-- L0107 --> <!-- L0109 --> <!-- L0110 -->

The lead archaeologist nodded toward the overhead rig, convinced that the fragment was the authentic keystone required to decode the inscription. "It confirms the reading," the scholar murmured, adjusting his spectacles. "Lower the rig." <!-- L0112 --> <!-- L0113 -->

With a low hydraulic hum, the mechanical claw descended from the ceiling, its steel pincers latching onto the reinforced glass vitrine. Gears clicked as the glass lifted, rising smoothly until a two-foot gap opened between the rim of the case and the museum floor. <!-- L0114 --> <!-- L0116 -->

Before the claw could clear another inch, the security detail flanking the perimeter moved. One of the men in matching dark suits stepped forward with practiced, military precision. Reaching inside his tailored blazer, he pulled out a heavy metallic cylinder, thumbed an activation switch, and hurled it under the rising glass. The device landed squarely atop the ancient tablet with a sharp metallic clatter, its digital face flashing with an eighteen-second countdown. <!-- L0118 -->

Around the gallery, the air grew suddenly thick and charged with ozone as the party took up defensive positions. The mundane illusion covering the security guards began to warp and dissolve like smoke in a draft. Beneath the harsh museum track lights, the neat crew-cuts on their heads began to writhe and hiss, their hair separating into dozens of living, coiling vipers. On their breast pockets, the silver museum security badges caught the fluorescent glare, each stamped with an identical name: *Gordon. Gordon. Gordon. Gordon.* <!-- L0142 --> <!-- L0145 --> <!-- L0147 --> <!-- L0148 -->

Pierre blinked, adjusting his round wire-rimmed spectacles as he took in the four identical nameplates.

"Hello," Pierre ventured in his lilting Parisian cadence, leaning forward across the handle of the archaeology cart. "Your name is very close to mine. Well, kind of. And you all have the exact same name. Is this your actual name?" <!-- L0155 -->

"This is our actual name, Gordon," the nearest operative replied, his voice grating with dry, reptilian sibilance as the snake crown reared back. <!-- L0156 --> <!-- L0158 -->

"We are sent to resolve the subjects at hand," Gordon announced, signaling to the other three. "Get the evidence first. Bring back the subjects alive." <!-- L0159 --> <!-- L0161 -->

"Oh dear," Pierre muttered, stepping back a pace. <!-- L0162 -->

The second Gordon drew a squat, pneumatic sidearm from his coat. Without hesitation, he pivoted and fired at an approaching museum attendant. A viscous pellet of inky, pitch-black liquid erupted from the muzzle, striking the bystander across the shoulder. The dark sludge hissed, spreading rapidly across the attendant's skin like spilled ink on blotting paper, calcifying the flesh beneath it into coarse gray stone. <!-- L0164 -->

Pierre stared at the spreading petrification, eyes wide with genuine scientific fascination. "Wait, how did you do that? I have been trying to do this! This is a very cool trick. Could I perhaps borrow your pistol, Mr. Gordon?" <!-- L0165 -->

"No," Gordon snapped coldly. "This is for Redactors only." <!-- L0166 -->

"Wait, what do I have to do to become a Redactor?" Pierre asked earnestly. <!-- L0167 --> <!-- L0169 -->

The operative's eyes narrowed into serpentine slits. "You are the subject. You will never be a Redactor." <!-- L0170 -->

Dravin took cover behind the base of the glass display claw, his university tweed rustling against the metal housing as he shouted a warning. <!-- L0172 -->

Pierre ducked low beside the rolling cart, turning to Alfie, who sat perched on the lower tray. "Alfie, I am not getting a very good impression from these new people here," Pierre whispered frantically. "Any ideas?" <!-- L0175 -->

"Yeah," Alfie growled, his hand tightening around the hilt of his tiny rapier. "He just pulled a gun out, so we better do something!" <!-- L0178 -->

"Yes, but it just turned him into stone," Pierre rationalized quickly. "It is not that big of a deal. There are a lot of other stone things in here—they get to be admired for many years, you know!" <!-- L0180 -->

"Yeah, well, I'm not really looking to become a piece of stone, mate!" Alfie barked back. "So we better do something!" <!-- L0181 -->

Pierre peered beneath the glass case. The digital device sat squarely atop the limestone tablet, beeping insistently as an oily black fluid began oozing across the ancient carvings. <!-- L0183 --> <!-- L0184 --> <!-- L0185 --> <!-- L0187 -->

"Alfie," Pierre murmured, a reckless grin spreading across his face. "Are you thinking what I am thinking?" <!-- L0188 --> <!-- L0189 --> <!-- L0191 --> <!-- L0192 -->

Rummaging through the archaeology cart's lower tool shelf, Pierre pushed past paintbrushes and specimen envelopes until his fingers wrapped around a bright orange, heavy-duty extension cord. <!-- L0193 --> <!-- L0194 --> <!-- L0196 --> <!-- L0197 -->

With quick, deft loops, Pierre fastened the heavy rubberized cable securely around the sturdy twine waistline of Alfie's coat. He scooped the driftwood doll up into his arms and dropped into a low crouch behind a wooden shipping crate, out of the Gorgons' direct line of fire. <!-- L0199 --> <!-- L0203 --> <!-- L0204 -->

"Okay, Alfie," Pierre briefed him in a rushed whisper. "I am going to tie this extension cord around you. And I have to admit, I am not very athletic. So plus or minus two feet is about all I can do for you. You may have to adjust in the air, but that is okay." <!-- L0206 -->

Alfie gave a determined nod of his carved wooden chin. "Yeah. All right. All right!" <!-- L0208 -->

Across the floor, the third Gordon sidestepped the stanchions and leveled his pneumatic pistol toward the reception lobby, aiming straight at Nincy who was filming with her ring light. <!-- L0210 --> <!-- L0211 --> <!-- L0212 --> <!-- L0213 -->

Gordon fired. The black viscous pellet struck Nincy square across her knuckles. The dark fluid surged outward in an instant, gluing her fingers together around the phone grip and creeping rapidly up her forearms in a sheath of rigid, petrifying gray stone. <!-- L0215 -->
