## CHAPTER 17: THE MIDNIGHT KNOCK & THE CORRECTOR DRILL

The group settled onto the cots, the warmth of the cabin soothing their road-weary bones. Outside, crickets chirped in rhythmic unison against the quiet mountain night. <!-- L0881 -->

Suddenly, the heavy cedar door slammed open. <!-- L0889 -->

Naomi barged into the room, kicking the door shut behind her with the heel of her boot. Her face was pale, her breath coming in tight, urgent gasps. <!-- L0897 -->

"Lights out!" Naomi hissed in a frantic whisper. "Kill the lanterns! Right now!" <!-- L0905 -->

Pierre scrambled to cup his hand over the kerosene lamp, pinching the wick until darkness enveloped the room. Through the glass panes, the warm ambient light of the settlement seemed to dim, as if swallowed by an encroaching shadow. <!-- L0921 -->

"Stay down on the floor," Naomi whispered from the corner, crouching low beside Dravin’s cot. "Corrector drill. Listen." <!-- L0954 -->

Outside the cabin, the crickets abruptly ceased their song. A heavy, suffocating silence pressed down on the roof. Through the window, faint wet slithering sounds dragged across the pine needles—a low, discordant hum that vibrated the glass panes. <!-- L0959 -->

Dravin held his breath, clutching his silver bell in his coat pocket. Beside him, Eusacles gripped the iron handle of his morningstar, ready to strike at the first shadow that crossed the threshold. <!-- L0961 -->

For three agonizing minutes, the cold planar presence lingered outside the cabin, circling the perimeter like a predator sniffing at closed shutters. Then, with a subtle shift in air pressure, the suffocating dread receded into the mountain woods. Outside, a solitary cricket chirped, followed by the soft sigh of the night breeze. <!-- L0990 -->

Naomi let out a long breath and struck a sulfur match, reigniting the lantern. "Three out of four surviving the drill without screaming. Not bad for newcomers." <!-- L1009 -->
