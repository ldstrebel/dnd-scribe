Late into the night, the newcomers prepared for bed inside the guest cabin, exhausted from planar transit. <!-- L0882 -->

Suddenly, Naomi barged in through the doorway, clutching a stack of rolled parchment.

Pierre paused mid-motion, clutching a small jar of cream with slices of cool cucumber balanced carefully across his eyelids. "It can wait until morning!" Pierre protested, his voice squeaking with indignation. "I have already started to do my face care! I only have so many cucumbers!" <!-- L0883 -->

"I am so sorry," Naomi stammered, caught off guard. "It's just—I get so locked in—" <!-- L0884 -->

"Yes, and it makes a lot of noise," Pierre insisted, taking a firm step toward the doorway. <!-- L0885 -->

Pierre gently and firmly swung the heavy cedar door shut on her. <!-- L0887 -->

"I'll be back in the morning!" Naomi called out through the timber planks. <!-- L0890 -->

"Yes, after eight you can come back," Pierre called back politely, latching the bolt. <!-- L0891 -->

The night passed in deep, undisturbed mountain silence. In the morning, a cheerful knock sounded at the cabin door. Mike stood on the sunlit porch holding an iron griddle and a steaming pot of coffee. <!-- L0900 -->

"What's up? Got Saturday pancakes going!" Mike announced brightly. "Coffee's hot. Come up to the inn. I know Naomi has been in your ear about checking out stuff, but eat first. We've been here for a century—there's no rush." <!-- L0901 -->

Pierre grinned eagerly, stepping out onto the porch. "I have heard Americans only like to work, work, work. For me, I am very excited to try these pancakes." <!-- L0902 -->

"People say I make the best," Mike laughed, "though there are only ten people here, so best out of ten isn't that impressive! Naomi's a bit of a workaholic. She got here in 2009, or was next in 2011, and hasn't stopped planning since." <!-- L0903 --> <!-- L0909 -->

Mike set down his iron spatula, leaning over the wooden counter. "Honestly, I have my own theory why we are all here," Mike confessed softly. "I am no demigod, but I have a card from my daughter that says To Dad. That concrete tether is keeping me anchored." <!-- L0921 --> <!-- L0922 -->

At the inn, breakfast was in full swing. Pierre took an eager bite of his pancake and chewed for a bit, only for dry crumbs to shower down over his plate as Mike sheepishly admitted the first batch was a little burnt. <!-- L0942 -->

As breakfast finished, Naomi marched into the dining hall, pointing purposefully at Pierre's wristwatch. "In Paris, it is already eleven o'clock!" Naomi declared triumphantly. "So I think this counts. It is time for you to come and see what I have found." <!-- L0954 -->

Pierre leaned across the table toward Professor Dravin, whispering behind his hand. "I am going to let you deal with the academics. I am going to take a nap in the back." Pierre gently pushed Edward forward. <!-- L0956 -->

Dravin raised his hands with an amused chuckle. "I was on my way to Las Vegas to study mythology, Pierre. I wouldn't lean too heavily on my academic abilities." <!-- L0959 -->

"Yes, but there is almost nothing she can say that will interest me," Pierre shrugged. "What if she starts talking about Zeus?" <!-- L0960 -->

"Ah, yes. Zeus," Dravin murmured with scholarly fondness. "I have met him many times in my books." <!-- L0961 -->

Pierre squinted through his spectacles, genuinely perplexed. "Wait... are you talking about Zeus or Seuss? Like, one is a doctor with rhyming cats, and one has lightning bolts?" <!-- L0962 -->

"Zeus," Dravin corrected firmly, adjusting his glasses. "Not Seuss." <!-- L0968 --> <!-- L0970 -->
