## CHAPTER 30: THE TEMPERED GLASS CASE

Pierre wheeled the cart directly up to the freestanding pedestal in the Aegean gallery. Behind the columns, Dravin and Eusacles kept watch on the hallway monitors. <!-- L1260 -->

Alfie popped his driftwood head out of Pierre's coat. "Keycard override on the lower right panel, Pierre." <!-- L1272 -->

Pierre slid the magnetic badge across the base sensor. *Click-whirrr*. The four magnetic locks retracted with a mechanical hiss, and the heavy tempered glass hood smoothly lifted upward on pneumatic pistons. <!-- L1275 -->

The moment the glass cleared the stone, the air in the gallery grew noticeably colder. The faint purple static around the Greek limestone flared brightly, humming with a frequency that resonated through the floorboards. <!-- L1281 -->

Pierre carefully lifted the heavy limestone stele onto the velvet-lined tray. Up close, the ancient Greek characters were mesmerizing—a rhythmic recounting of heroes, lineages, and severed destinies. <!-- L1311 -->

"Curator said this was dug up in Thessaly," Alfie whispered, tapping the jagged fracture. "Look at the break line." <!-- L1331 -->

Pierre reached into his canvas bag and retrieved the palm-sized limestone fragment he had carried from the Margin. The weathered grain, the mineral veins, and the sharp jagged edges aligned with mathematical perfection. <!-- L1338 -->

"It was never lost," Pierre realized with quiet awe. "It was severed. Cut out by the Fates when they edited this mortal king out of history." <!-- L1344 -->

"And now we mend the weave," Dravin said softly as he stepped out from the colonnade to join them. <!-- L1346 -->

Pierre positioned the stone fragment above the void, his hands steadying against the ancient rock. <!-- L1379 -->

"Let's see what the truth says," Eusacles nodded. <!-- L1381 -->
