## CHAPTER 11: THE BONFIRE AT BLOCK 99

Night fell over Vumbua with the sudden, damp chill of the northern sea. Across the sprawling basin, the repurposed expedition campus transformed into a labyrinth of shadows and glowing sparks. Seventy thousand candidates, adrift in the bewildering sprawl of the academy on their first night, had begun congregating in pockets of warmth. Wherever the turf had not yet taken root or where timber scraps lay stacked beside unfinished scaffolds, fires were being raised against the dark. <!-- L0306 --> <!-- L0308 -->

Above them, the sky was alive with movement. Airships with glowing signal lanterns glided on silent thermal currents between the spires, their hulls gleaming brass-yellow against the stars, while down in the basin, cutters and steam launches carved white wakes across the black water. From the western bluffs, the sound of orchestral music and bright glass lanterns drifted down from the grand manors where aristocratic scions held court. But down in the dust of the lower quads, the common candidates made do with construction scrap and camaraderie. <!-- L0310 -->

Ignatius stood in the shadows near Block 99, black traveling coat dusted with chalky soot, watching the small clusters of students pass by. <!-- L0312 --> <!-- L0313 -->

A few paces away, Lomi approached the circle, flat cap pushed back on his brow, wiping machine grease from his knuckles with a rag. He had spent the last twenty minutes observing a growing crisis at the central pit. <!-- L0314 --> <!-- L0315 --> <!-- L0316 -->

"Hey, Ignatius," Lomi called out with an easy grin, nodding back over his shoulder toward the industrial yard. "Good to see you again. They could use a little help over by the pit. I don't mean to insinuate anything, but I think you could get a fire going if you needed to." <!-- L0317 --> <!-- L0320 --> <!-- L0322 --> <!-- L0327 --> <!-- L0335 --> <!-- L0337 -->

Ignatius crossed his arms, raising a solitary eyebrow while tiny crimson embers danced lazily along the fringe of his hair. "Let me get this straight. You think that just because I come from the Ember Isles that you can just stroll over here and ask me for a light whenever you need one? You're not wrong, but that's not the point." <!-- L0338 -->

Lomi chuckled, pulling a dry twist of waste paper from his canvas pocket. "I wasn't saying you had to perform. Just figured I'd bring a scrap of paper and a polite request." <!-- L0341 --> <!-- L0343 -->

"Well, honestly," Ignatius said, the corner of his mouth ticking upward, "I'm glad you trust me so much right out of the gate." <!-- L0344 -->

"There's just something about you that screams 'good at starting fires,'" Lomi said. "Can't imagine why." <!-- L0345 -->

Ignatius took the paper twist, letting the warmth of his thumb char the edge into a steady, glowing coal. "Yeah, yeah. Sure. Let's go see what they're struggling with." <!-- L0346 -->

They strolled over to the fire pit, where a crowd of shivering candidates had gathered around a nervous young student in thick brass spectacles. The boy was kneeling in the dirt, clutching a clipboard covered in manic geometric diagrams, adjusting every piece of lumber with millimeter precision. He had laid out a hyper-symmetrical pyramid of dry pine, but refused to let anyone near it until his draft calculations balanced. <!-- L0349 --> <!-- L0351 -->

Ignatius didn't wait for the math. With a casual flick of his wrist, he tossed the burning scrap directly into the heart of the kindling. <!-- L0352 -->

A hungry *whoosh* tore through the yard. The pine caught instantly, leaping five feet into the air in a roaring geyser of orange sparks.

The bespectacled boy shrieked, tumbling backward onto his elbows. "No! No, no, no! The draft was calibrated for ignition from the western quadrant! What is this? This is entirely non-optimal!" He scrambled to his feet, squinting through the heat at Lomi. "Explain yourself, please! Why have you done this?"

Lomi rested his hands on his hips, totally unfazed by the academic outrage. "In my experience down in the boiler rooms, every minute a furnace isn't burning is money out of your pocket. Time is money, kid. You focus on uptime, whether it's pretty or not. Perfect is the enemy of good. We needed a fire, now we got a fire." <!-- L0354 --> <!-- L0356 -->

The boy stared at the leaping flames, his mouth opening and closing like a landed perch. He looked down at his ruined clipboard, then back at the roaring blaze, grudgingly adjusting his spectacles. "An interesting proposition. I will... consider this. It was effective, certainly, though it is not always true that momentum is life, regardless of what the radio announcers preach." He cleared his throat, straightening his crumpled tunic. "I am Cassius. Given that you have successfully concluded an operational objective, you are entitled to know." <!-- L0359 --> <!-- L0373 -->

"Lomi," the mechanic replied, taking the boy's hand with a firm, calloused grip. "Worked down in the Deep-Hull's boilers for years. It's nice to see what you boys are designing up here top-side." <!-- L0374 --> <!-- L0376 --> <!-- L0378 -->

"I am merely a first-year student," Cassius mumbled, cheeks flushing a violent crimson as his gaze darted past Lomi toward the edge of the courtyard. "I... I just felt this was the place to be tonight." <!-- L0379 --> <!-- L0402 -->

Lomi caught the boy's furtive glance instantly. Standing thirty paces back near the crane footings was Serra Vox, clutching her field notes and observing the fire with bright, predatory curiosity. Lomi clapped Cassius hard across the shoulder, speaking in a hearty, booming voice designed to carry. "Well, man, I don't see how we would've gotten warmth tonight without you! You laid a great foundation, Cassius! Beautiful work!" <!-- L0403 --> <!-- L0406 -->

Cassius stumbled forward from the force of the pat, beaming with flustered pride. <!-- L0411 -->

At the edge of the light, Britt and Aggie watched the blaze with quiet wariness. To beings of bark, soil, and living mycelium, a roaring fire was never a simple comfort; it was an ancient predator that consumed the forest floor. Aggie hovered just behind Britt's shoulder, her dark eyes tracking the leaping sparks with cautious fascination. <!-- L0417 -->

Serra Vox, meanwhile, had crept up beside them, practically vibrating with anthropological excitement. She chewed her lip, opened her mouth to ask a question, hesitated, and fidgeted with her pen. <!-- L0421 --> <!-- L0423 -->

"Are you all right?" Britt asked, turning a cool, level gaze upon her. <!-- L0426 -->

"Oh gosh, is it that obvious?" Serra blurted out, her composure crumbling into breathless enthusiasm. "I'm sorry, I really didn't mean to stare! It's just... this is the most remarkable gathering of disparate cultures since the Treaty of the Spires! I'm completely overwhelmed. Where are you from? I mean—have you seen maps of the continent? Oh, that was rude, I didn't mean to assume—" <!-- L0427 -->

"We've seen some maps," Britt answered evenly. "Our clan lives in the southern canopies. We don't get much outside news." <!-- L0430 -->

"Well, the world is enormous!" Serra explained, gesturing broadly toward the north. "The Seat of Harmony alone holds eight million citizens! It's colossal—far too noisy for my taste, honestly, but it's where all the great expeditions were launched. That's why being here at Vumbua feels so historic. We're on the edge of a new age!" <!-- L0431 --> <!-- L0433 --> <!-- L0435 -->
