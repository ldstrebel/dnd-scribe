## CHAPTER 16: STANFORD TWEED AND DRIFTWOOD MEMORIES

As the night deepened, Teddy guided the group past the split-rail fences toward a cluster of rustic timber cabins nestled among the pines. The settlement felt timeless—a quiet haven of oil lanterns, hand-hewn logs, and hand-stitched quilts. <!-- L0760 -->

Pierre walked beside the professor, admiring the peaceful forest air. "I must say, Monsieur Dravin, despite the ink beasts, I find this place quite lovely." <!-- L0769 -->

"There is an extraordinary amount of Greek mythology tucked away in unexpected corners of America," Dravin noted, glancing up through the pine boughs. "Even in Las Vegas—between the marble replicas at Caesars Palace and the neon sphinxes—the archetypes persist. Myth has a way of anchoring itself in cultural memory." <!-- L0790 -->

"I never had much time for fancy universities like Stanford," Teddy remarked as he led them up the wooden porch of the main cabin. "Spent most of my youth on the timber trails. But out here, you learn that every myth was just history before someone decided to edit the manuscript." <!-- L0809 -->

Inside the cabin, an older woman named Rosa was arranging handmade wool blankets on cedar cots. When she caught sight of Alfie balanced neatly on Pierre’s shoulder, her eyes lit up with gentle curiosity. <!-- L0833 -->

"And who might you be, little traveler?" Rosa asked gently, offering a wooden stool. <!-- L0849 -->

Alfie hopped down onto the rustic pine table, adjusting the sewing-needle rapier at his hip. "Name's Alfie, ma'am. Driftwood Duelist, at your service. Last thing I remember before waking up in that marble palace was darkness... salt water... the roar of cannon fire, and blood on the deck. Next thing I know, I'm carved out of driftwood with needle and thread." <!-- L0873 -->
