## CHAPTER 32: THE CRACKLING VEIL & THE INK-BLOT PROBE

The sharp, piercing shriek of the fire alarm tore through the lecture hall, vibrating through the wooden floorboards and setting the overhead fixtures rattling. Cold air rushed through the high auditorium as panicked students scrambled over desks, knocking down folding chairs in their frantic race toward the lobby. A blinding burst of ozone had erupted the moment Edward Dravin turned the brass key in Dr. Thorne's briefcase, snapping the party out of the 1948 laboratory vision and dropping them straight into chaos. <!-- L0217 -->

In the second row of the steeply raked seating, the quiet woman in the tailored coat rose to her feet. The mundane human glamour melted from her skin like wax over an open flame. Thick dark hair began swirling around her head in a suspended tempest, crackling with arcs of blue-white electrical charge that hummed against the linoleum. Her slitted, predatory eyes swept the stage, locking onto the open briefcase and the ancient buckram binder inside. Surrounding students froze in place, caught in a heavy, dazing field of suppression that left their limbs moving in sluggish, dreamlike slow motion. At the three main entrances to the auditorium, heavy double doors banged against the wall stops as three towering, cloaked figures strode inward, cloven hooves thudding on the tiles. <!-- L0218 -->

Pierre adjusted his round wire spectacles, ducking behind the wooden podium as a barrage of papers fluttered through the draft. He gripped the smooth shaft of his bronze museum javelin, taking in the tactical layout: exits blocked, horned infiltrators flanking the aisles, and an unnatural rift tearing through the plaster wall near the green room. <!-- L0240 -->

Pierre raised an open palm from behind the podium, attempting an urgent diplomatic smile. "Excuse me. Excuse me, madame. We could have had a pleasant conversation, you know. We saw your satyr companions with the Fates—we spoke with them! We could be friends." <!-- L0258 -->

From the second row, the crackling woman tilted her head, her levitating coils of hair hissing with raw planar voltage as she fixed her gaze on Pierre's black woolen beret.

"I certainly know you," she said, her voice resonant with unnatural double harmonics that echoed over the blaring siren. "I think I will just take the information from your brain. How does that sound?" <!-- L0259:woman -->

"No," Pierre countered smoothly in his crisp Parisian drawl, his jaw tightening as he stepped out from behind the mahogany lectern. "I do not like that at all, actually." He glanced over his shoulder toward the rear wall, where reality buckled into a dark dimensional seam. "Three satyrs at the doors, and now another rift behind us." <!-- L0260 -->

The dark fissure shuddered. With a wet, sucking plop, a fist-sized globular mass of viscous black ink dropped through the rift and hit the floor, undulating like a living blot. <!-- L0261 -->

"Professor, do you have a plan for this?" Pierre called out, vaulting the low partition railing and skipping across the steps to keep clear of the dazed aura radiating from the second row. <!-- L0264 -->

Panicked stragglers shoved past him in a desperate press toward the exits, forcing Pierre to brace his shoulder against the end of an oak desk to keep his footing as he pushed toward the edge of the stage. <!-- L0271 -->

Right in front of his boots, the ink blot quivered. Pierre halted, extended his arms, and carefully leaned forward. Using the blunt butt-end of his javelin, he gently prodded the glistening dark mass. "You seem to have a little problem," Pierre murmured with aristocratic disdain. <!-- L0272 -->

The black substance immediately surged upward, molding around the wooden haft of the spear like adhesive tar and clinging with surprising weight. <!-- L0273 -->

Pierre arched an eyebrow, pivoting on his heel. "If you wish to stick to my spear, very well," he muttered, drawing the weapon back like a lacrosse stick. "Let us see how you like the aisle." With a sharp flick of his wrists, he whipped the javelin forward. <!-- L0274 -->

The ink blot tore free with a wet slap, arcing through the air across the raked rows of the auditorium. It sailed wide of the crackling woman, descending into the fleeing crowd along the eastern steps. <!-- L0286 -->

The tar-like blot landed squarely against the shoulder of a fleeing attendant. The creature clung fast, spreading sticky tendrils across the fabric. The terrified man shrieked, batting wildly at his shoulder in a dazed panic until the creature released its grip, leaping off his coat and landing with a dull thud atop an empty wooden student desk. <!-- L0292 -->

Pierre wrinkled his nose in profound culinary disgust, shaking the butt of his spear. "I do not know what this is, but you have clearly overcooked it! You should have decreased the heat on the oven. This is like a wheel of burnt molasses!" <!-- L0296 -->

Across the hall, the panicked crowd continued to surge toward the exits, though anyone passing within fifteen feet of the transformed woman slowed to a dazed, stumbling shuffle under her psychic aura. <!-- L0297 -->

At the back of the auditorium, the heavy emergency exit doors remained thrown wide open, where the three horned satyrs stood sentinel, blocking the corridors and cutting off the easiest route to the street. <!-- L0301 -->

Outside in the lobby, the demonstrators who had spent the morning wearing folded tin-foil fedoras tossed their discarded disguises onto the tile floor. As their cloaks parted, coarse fur and muscular beastlike frames emerged beneath the overhead lights. <!-- L0314 -->

Up on the stage, Alfie sat upright atop the buckram medical binder inside Dr. Thorne's open briefcase. Her painted seashell eyes darted from the wailing fire strobe to the crackling woman in row two, her tiny wooden hand reaching instinctively for her sewing-needle rapier. Beside her, Dr. Thorne sat frozen against the desk in stark disbelief, while Professor Dravin stood over the podium, clutching his silver pocketwatch and ancient hoplite shield. <!-- L0315 --> <!-- L0317 -->

Alfie adjusted her green-and-white soccer scarf and gave her oversized souvenir museum ballcap a resolute tug. "Right then," the driftwood figurine rasped, patting the braided leather strap at her waist. "I thought I'd go with the chaos belt." <!-- L0318 -->

She reached for the engraved bronze buckle, preparing to unleash three random relics into the fray. <!-- L0340 -->
