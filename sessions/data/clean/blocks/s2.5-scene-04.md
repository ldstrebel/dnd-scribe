## CHAPTER 19: SLIPPING AWAY FROM THE BONFIRE

The laughter around the pit rose in volume as the pine wood caught fire, bathing the candidates in a fierce, crackling amber glow. Ignatius was bounding forward with a grin, hair trailing bright red cinders, calling out for everyone to step closer and feel the heat. Iggy noticed that Ignatius glanced back over his shoulder, his glowing eyes scanning the perimeter until they found Iggy's trench coat. Ignatius waved him forward, an earnest invitation to join the inner circle. Iggy wasn't forgotten. For a moment, the warmth of the gesture rooted him to the flagstones. <!-- L0285 --> <!-- L0288 --> <!-- L0290 -->

Then the crowd surged. Dozens of students pushed in, eager to claim a spot near the flames. Conversations collided in an impenetrable wall of academic chatter. Near the kindling, that bespectacled boy Cassius was loudly debating draft airflow while trying, rather pathetically, to flirt with Sarah Vox, who looked far more interested in taking structural notes than listening to his romantic overtures. Further back, a small cluster of Mazizi cadets stood in quiet contemplation, speaking in low, reverent tones about how death was an inevitable and beautiful necessity of life. Iggy listened for thirty seconds, blinked behind his copper lenses, and decided he had reached his social limit for the evening. <!-- L0320 --> <!-- L0327 --> <!-- L0353 --> <!-- L0357 --> <!-- L0373 --> <!-- L0385 -->

He looked to his left. Just beyond the perimeter of the courtyard, past the unfinished colonnade, stood a massive, utilitarian structure of black basalt and riveted iron plates. An arched lintel above the double bronze doors bore a bold, cast-iron inscription: *L-POWER*. <!-- L0388 --> <!-- L0395 --> <!-- L0400 -->

As Iggy watched, the heavy doors groaned open. A silhouette emerged into the cool evening air—an engineer in soot-stained canvas overalls, carrying a toolbox. For two brief seconds, the interior of the building was laid bare to the courtyard: the rhythmic thrum of colossal spinning cogs, the deep, resonant hum of pressurized turbines, and the pulsing, ethereal blue luminescence of energy conduits tracing along the high rafters. As the technician walked away, the bronze door swung shut behind him with a dull thud, bouncing slightly on its frame. It didn't latch. The deadbolt hadn't caught.

To anyone else, it was an unremarkable maintenance access. To Iggy, it was an open invitation written in grease and gears. He didn't say goodbye. He didn't announce his departure to Ignatius or Lomi or anyone else. He simply took two quiet steps backward into the long shadow cast by the crane's foundation, turned his collar up, and melted away from the bonfire.
