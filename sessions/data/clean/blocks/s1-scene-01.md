## CHAPTER 1: THE DESERT TRANSIT

The air conditioning inside the transit bus had died somewhere between the outskirts of Henderson and the endless, baking flats of the Nevada desert. <!-- L0958 -->

Outside the dust-streaked windows, waves of heat shimmered over cracked asphalt, warped by the relentless afternoon sun. Inside, the cabin smelled of sun-baked vinyl, stale motor oil, and overheated machinery. The suspension squealed in agony with every dip in the road, rattling the luggage racks overhead. <!-- L0959 -->

Near the back, Pierre shifted uncomfortably against the cracked seat. He adjusted his spectacles, his fingers instinctively brushing the rim of his woolen beret to make sure it hadn't slipped. He pulled his canvas rucksack tighter against his chest, staring with deep suspicion at the barren, flat wasteland stretching to the horizon. America was too big, too empty, and entirely too hot.

Suspended from the low ceiling at the front of the aisle, a boxy CRT television flickered through static, droning out a tinny midday broadcast. <!-- L0960 -->

"...sweltering heat and high desert winds continue to break records across the Southwest," the news anchor’s monotone voice buzzed through the speaker. "In national news, authorities confirm that the commercial flight bound for the capital has landed safely outside D.C. We turn now to our field reporter—" <!-- L0961 -->

The broadcast was instantly drowned out by a deafening, metallic shriek. <!-- L0962 -->

Pierre gasped as the bus lurched violently to the left. Beneath the floorboards, the rear tires lost all traction on the superheated asphalt. Rubber smoked and spun freely, screaming as the chassis fishtailed. <!-- L0963 -->

Slam. <!-- L0964 -->

The violent torque tossed passengers across the narrow aisle. Luggage tumbled from the overhead racks, clattering against seats and skulls. <!-- L0965 -->

"Hold on!" someone shouted over the screech of twisting steel.

Slam. <!-- L0966 -->

Pierre’s knuckles turned white as he clamped onto the handrail, but physics had other plans. In an instant, the crushing G-forces vanished, replaced by a stomach-dropping, sickening sensation of total weightlessness. Pierre floated out of his seat, his beret fluttering, rucksack drifting in mid-air as the world tumbled upside down.

Slam. <!-- L0967 -->

Before anyone could scream, a blinding explosion of searing heat and white light detonated across the windows. The walls of the bus dissolved into radiant phosphor. The screams, the roaring engine, and the scent of burning rubber were swallowed by absolute, ringing silence.

"Wow..." Pierre breathed softly into the void. <!-- L0968 -->

And then, the light consumed everything.
