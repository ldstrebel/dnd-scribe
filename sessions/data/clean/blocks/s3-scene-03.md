## CHAPTER 25: THE FRACTURED STELE

They passed under an arched entryway into the darkened Aegean gallery. Spotlights illuminated marble busts of philosophers, bronze hoplite helmets, and rows of terracotta amphorae. <!-- L0600 -->

At the center of the gallery stood an isolated, freestanding pedestal encased in heavy three-quarter-inch tempered glass. Inside, resting on black velvet, was a slab of weathered grey limestone roughly two feet across, etched with deep archaic Greek characters. <!-- L0620 -->

"There it is," Eusacles breathed, stepping closer to the velvet stanchions. <!-- L0640 -->

The tablet was severely fractured along its upper-right corner. A jagged, triangular void broke the continuity of the inscription, leaving several lines of ancient verse sheared in half. Around the display case, a faint, almost imperceptible shimmer of purple static buzzed in the air—a subtle planar vibration that made Dravin's silver bell hum in his pocket. <!-- L0660 -->

Pierre leaned in, peering through his spectacles at the plaque: *UNIDENTIFIED HELLENIC FRAGMENT (CIRCA 450 BCE). EXCAVATED FROM ANOMALOUS STRATA.* <!-- L0680 -->

"The stone matches the piece in my rucksack," Pierre whispered, touching the canvas bag where the jagged limestone fragment was wrapped in oilcloth. "The fracture pattern is identical." <!-- L0700 -->

"And the display case is wired with magnetic contact sensors," Dravin noted, pointing to the discreet copper contacts along the steel frame. "Lifting that glass triggers a building-wide alarm." <!-- L0720 -->
