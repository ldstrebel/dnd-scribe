## CHAPTER 28: THE WALK TO THE CELESTIAL LOUNGE & CARTOGRAPHY

The night deepened past two in the morning, turning the damp streets of Vumbua quiet and still. Most of the seventy thousand candidates had long since collapsed into their temporary barracks or huddled beside dying embers in the lower quads. But for a scattered few, the restless thrum of an unfamiliar world kept sleep at bay. <!-- L0013 --> <!-- L0014 --> <!-- L0053 -->

Loami led the squad along the curving flagstone terraces toward the Upper Core, his flat cap tilted back, hands shoved deep into his pockets. "I've spent ten years working the swing shift in the Deep-Hull's boilers," he said with an easy shrug. "Two in the morning is just when the steam pressure settles. I'm a night owl." <!-- L0054 -->

Beside him, Ignatius walked with buoyant, tireless strides, tiny sparks flickering at the tips of his fingers. "Everything is too new to sleep," he said, dark eyes gleaming. "You don't cross the ocean just to lie in a cot."

Britt, however, trailed half a step behind, her bark-grained shoulders stiff, dark amber eyes heavy with fatigue. Back in the ancient groves of the Mazizi, life followed the sun; rising with dawn and resting when twilight veiled the canopy was sacred order. Staying awake in an artificial city lit by humming resonance lamps felt perverse. Aggie hovered beside her, sensing her sister's prickly reluctance, while Iggy trotted quietly in the rear, his woolen trench coat buttoned to his chin and round goggles reflecting the gaslights.

At the crest of the limestone bluff stood the Celestial Lounge. Outside its arched mahogany doors, a tall man in an immaculate black butler's livery stood guard, brass buttons gleaming in the torchlight. He eyed their dirt-dusted boots and grease-stained cuffs with aristocratic detachment, but as Loami stepped forward with easy confidence, the attendant merely bowed and pushed the double doors wide. <!-- L0055 --> <!-- L0056 -->

The upper floor opened into an expansive, glass-domed observatory. Gaslights burning behind tinted vanilla crystal cast warm, amber shadows across velvet chaises and low mahogany tables. Across the room, exhausted scions and upperclassmen lay sprawled in slumber, half-empty crystal flutes scattered on sideboards. But it was not the luxury that took the squad's breath away; it was the panoramic glass wall curving along the eastern perimeter. <!-- L0057 --> <!-- L0058 --> <!-- L0059 --> <!-- L0060 -->

Loami and Britt were drawn to the glass as if pulled by gravity. Below them, the sprawling harbor complex stretched across the basin in an intricate grid of steam and lantern light. The massive silhouette of the Deep-Hull was anchored in the harbor basin, steam stacks venting white plumes against the black sea. To the west, the glowing envelope of the Sky Spire hung suspended in the clouds, tethered by steel cables that gleamed like spider silk. Half a million lives, countless lanterns, and endless industrial conduits hummed beneath them. <!-- L0061 --> <!-- L0062 --> <!-- L0063 --> <!-- L0064 -->

"Look at that," Loami murmured, pressing a calloused palm against the cold glass. "That's where the steam lines run. That's the power plant down by Block 99. You can see the whole layout from up here... like looking down at a living blueprint." <!-- L0066 -->

Aggie stood beside Britt, her lips parting in silent awe. "Whoa..." she whispered, the sheer scale of surface civilization pressing upon her senses like a physical weight.

Ignatius didn't just look—he immediately unbuttoned his traveler's tunic, pulled out his leatherbound field notebook, and began sketching furiously. Beside him, Iggy hopped onto a cushioned stool, using the elevated vantage to trace the transit roads, harbor locks, and structural conduits Loami pointed out. In twenty minutes of quiet, feverish scribbling, the two of them mapped the topography of the spires with remarkable precision. <!-- L0067 --> <!-- L0068 --> <!-- L0069 -->

"Hey! You!" a loud, cheerful voice called across the lounge. <!-- L0070 -->

Ignatius looked up. Striding toward them was an aristocratic young man in an embroidered silver-and-black tunic, waving with exuberant familiarity. It was Valentine "Val" Sterling—the cadet Ignatius had encountered during the entrance examination. Behind him, seated near a sound-baffled broadcast studio lined with audio crystals, was Ember, Ignatius's sharp-tongued Ash-Blood cousin, observing the room with cool, calculating detachment. <!-- L0071 --> <!-- L0075 --> <!-- L0080 -->
