## CHAPTER 24: RECONNAISSANCE IN THE MEDITERRANEAN WING

Inside the climate-controlled galleries, the ambient murmur of museum visitors echoed off marble floors. Dravin led the group through the dinosaur rotunda and up the wide central staircase toward the second-floor Mediterranean wing. <!-- L0470 -->

Pierre kept a watchful eye on the security grid. High in the corners of each gallery, dome-mounted CCTV cameras swiveled on mechanical servos, tracking the crowd. Security guards in crisp black uniforms and golden badges stood stationed at key doorways, radios clipped to their duty belts. <!-- L0490 -->

"Four guards on this floor," Eusacles whispered, leaning casually against an informational kiosk. "Two by the atrium stairs, one roaming the Roman pottery room, and one stationed directly at the special exhibit entrance." <!-- L0510 -->

Alfie peeked out from Pierre's breast pocket, his tiny shell eyes scanning the high plaster moldings. "Ventilation shafts look plenty wide for a doll with a needle, mate. If doors get locked, I've got a highway in the ceiling." <!-- L0530 -->

Dravin nodded thoughtfully, inspecting the promotional brochure. "The exhibit features a private research wing behind the main hall. That is where they keep recently cataloged items undergoing linguistic translation." <!-- L0550 -->

"The Greek tablet," Pierre murmured, his pulse quickening. "Naomi said it was found only three months ago. They are still trying to read it." <!-- L0570 -->

"Because the grammar makes no sense to modern historians," Dravin explained. "When an edit occurs in history, the language on the stone reflects a timeline that never reached consensus. To them, it is gibberish. To the Fates, it is an open wound." <!-- L0590 -->
