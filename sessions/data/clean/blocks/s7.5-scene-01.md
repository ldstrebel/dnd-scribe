# Chapter 74: The Mycelium Dampener & The Diplomat's Deck

The heavy oak doors of Sterling Hall creaked shut behind Aggie, muffling the lingering murmurs of the afternoon lecture. Thursday sunlight spilled across the cobblestones of the courtyard, where cadets and mechanics hurried toward the harbor and the colonnade. Aggie adjusted her dark traveler’s pack, her quiet eyes sweeping the stone perimeter with the sharp instinct of an unburdened scout beneath the open sky, tracking the atmospheric charge that always preceded a major festival. Before she could plot a path toward the dormitories, an unmistakable whirlwind descended upon the path.

Laden with bulging canvas satchels, dried root bundles dangling from leather straps, and pockets overflowing with fresh aromatic herbs, Angela Galaspora bustled through the archway. A crown of living woven green leaves rested lopsided upon her graying hair. She beamed with uncontainable warmth, waving both hands vigorously as she ambushed Aggie in the open court.

"Hey girls! Just the Mizizi I was looking for. Come with me—we have a day ahead!" <!-- L0027 -->

Aggie exchanged a quick, knowing glance with Britt, whose green woven tunic rustled as she tried to match the elder’s relentless pace. Aunt Angela was an elemental force of nature; resisting her was like trying to redirect a summer thunderstorm. Nodding, Aggie fell into step beside the botanist, letting the elder herd them through the university’s iron gates and toward the sprawling thoroughfare.

The crisp white marble pillars of The Colonnade soon rose before them, framing a wide panoramic view of the canyon below. Sunlight glinted off polished brass balustrades and the massive suspension cradles of the airship berths. Far below in the half-mile basalt chasm of the Apex Ring, the fairgrounds swarmed with thousands of spectators. Team pennants whipped in the canyon gusts, while the deep, resonant hum of heavy motor blocks and the high-pitched whine of next-generation harmony batteries vibrated through the stone floorboards. Vendors threaded through the throngs hoisting backpack dispensers of spiced cider and cider-grog.

As they paused near the ramp of the VIP berth, Aunt Angela leaned in close to Aggie, lowering her voice to an urgent murmur. Her sharp eyes darted toward Britt, who lagged half a step behind with slightly bowed shoulders.

"Is Britt okay, child? She looks... slouched. Out of breath." <!-- L0045 -->

A spark of apprehension flickered through Aggie’s mind like lightning behind cloud cover. Focusing her gaze, she stepped closer to inspect her friend. Britt’s breathing was noticeably shallow, strained and uneven against the mountain air. Worse, the delicate velvet fungal blooms along her carapace appeared brittle and parchment-dry, lacking their usual vibrant moisture. Aggie’s fingers hovered near the dried patches, sensing deep discordance within the living tissue. While the exact chemical nature of the botanical toxin remained unfamiliar, Aggie knew beyond all doubt that an unnatural affliction had taken root.

Aggie turned to Aunt Angela, keeping her voice low and tight so as not to alarm the passing socialites. She described the dry desiccation on the shell, the unnatural exhaustion, and the constricted rhythm in the lungs.

Angela’s face tightened into deep, troubled furrows as she gently took Britt’s fingers into her own, turning them over in the daylight.

"I thought she looked strange. She does not look too bad, but I get a strange feeling of deja vu looking at her right now, though I can't place it. I need my books—you must find me in the forest after exams!" <!-- L0056 -->

The elder offered a steadying squeeze, urging them onward toward the gangway before lingering eyes could notice the tension. They crossed the boarding ramp and stepped onto the grand observation deck of the *Zephyr*.

The flagship vessel was a triumphant display of cutting-edge Harmony engineering and luxury. Warm, polished mahogany planking gleamed under rich gaslight lanterns, and ornate brass railings bounded the perimeter. At the center of the promenade, a colossal circular floor of reinforced crystal looked straight down a mile into the dizzying depths of the basalt canyon floor.

Standing near the entryway to welcome dignitaries was Valentine Sterling Sr., the renowned explorer who had first mapped the Mizizi territories. He wore a crisp field blazer and held an open leather journal covered in handwritten vehicle schematics and canyon drafts. Beside him stood Cade Ashveil, a tall, composed Ash-blood with the proud posture of his volcanic ancestry.

Aunt Angela immediately surged forward into the diplomatic reception, beaming as if no shadow hung over the day.

"Ah Mr. Cade, thank you thank you for arranging this adventure and pass my thanks along to Lady Ignis! Mr. Sterling, it is of course fantastic to see you again. I am so excited to see this ship fly and to learn about this Resy Race." <!-- L0063 -->

The explorer offered an amused, polite chuckle, bowing his head slightly as he adjusted his spectacles.

"Hello Elder Angela, welcome to the Zephyr, please note it is a Reso race not resy, but I am very excited for this season to begin—do you know any of the racers?" <!-- L0068 -->

Angela’s hands fluttered as she gestured vaguely toward the chasm, her thoughts already flitting across the ship’s elaborate decor.

"Well I think our fellow Mycelium Circle member Rill had something to do with one of the teams, but honestly I forget which—wow look at these ferns...!" <!-- L0072 -->

The elder pivoted abruptly toward a massive, cascading urn of exotic botanical fronds lining the entryway. As Sterling smiled knowingly and began guiding them inside toward the hospitality lounges, Cade Ashveil cleared his throat, attempting to bridge the polite social pause.

"Well these ferns are great, almost as cool as the rocks out in the race." <!-- L0076 -->

Aggie nodded courteously, pointing out how dense cliffside foliage could easily mask an overtaking crawler on the track’s blind switchbacks, drawing an approving nod from the Ash-blood officer. Leaving the elder botanist and the dignitaries to their pleasantries, Aggie drifted along the perimeter of the passenger deck.

Liveried stewards carrying silver trays of delicate appetizers and bubbling refreshments glided between clusters of aristocrats and academy patrons. Toward the refreshment tables, Aggie spotted two familiar first-year cadets: Pip, frantically stuffing pockets full of sweet sky-bites, and Bramble, a towering, gentle youth who stood clutching an immense, leather-bound manual on structural mycelium physiology with an expression of pure dread.

Aggie approached them with a quiet smile. Seeing Bramble’s exhaustion, she pulled a compact primer on aero-static lift and glider balance from her satchel, offering an impromptu trade. Bramble’s eyes lit up with profound relief as he practically thrust the heavy Mycelium treatise into her hands, eager to escape botanical theory for basic aeronautics.

Tucking herself into a quiet corner, Aggie leafed through Bramble’s dense, margin-crowded notes. Her sharp gaze picked out crucial scribbles detailing the extraction of active dampening enzymes from local root fibers. Reading between the hasty ink lines, she reconstructed the forgotten schematic for brewing a potent Mycelium Spore-Dampener—a vital counter-agent capable of soothing acute fungal stress.

Pocketing the breakthrough, Aggie slipped past the busy dining saloon into the stateroom corridor. Standing alone by a wide panoramic viewing pane was Valentine Sterling Jr. The young heir turned as she approached, his shoulders relaxing as he recognized a trusted classmate.

"I'm glad you're here. I wanted to pick you guys up from the dorms myself, but my father wouldn't let me leave the ship. He sent Ludo instead... who I see is currently enjoying his off-duty status at the bar." <!-- L0090 -->

Aggie paused beside him, murmuring an inquiry about tomorrow’s entrance trials. Val glanced down the carpeted hall, leaning in close.

"Look, you need to be ready. I plan to lock myself in my room to study tonight. My father won't say much, but there is something after the written test though even my father won't tell me what. He's fair if nothing else." <!-- L0095 -->

Aggie offered a firm nod of gratitude, committing the warning to memory. As she made her way back to rejoin Aunt Angela, the deep blast of the first ceremonial race horn reverberated through the hull, announcing that the trials of the day had officially begun.
