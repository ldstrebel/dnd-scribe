## CHAPTER 25: THE SHIP OF MANY OARS

Iggy stared down at his boots, thinking of the dark galleries of the Trench. "If a place isn't part of Harmony yet... does it still feel the pull?" <!-- L1001 --> <!-- L1002 -->

The professor grabbed his chalk, swung around, and slammed his cane against the drafting table. "Think of Harmony not as a power plant, but as a colossal galley upon an open sea. Every integrated territory is an oarsman seated upon the bench. Some oars are pulled by towering giants who drive the ship forward with ferocious strength; others are pulled by smaller craftsmen. When the Ash-Bloods sat at the bench, every demographic calculation insisted they would pull like a titan. Instead, they barely pulled with the strength of an infant. Yet the ship moves forward, and the wake it leaves behind disturbs the waters for leagues around." <!-- L1010 --> <!-- L1020 --> <!-- L1030 --> <!-- L1032 -->

"The elders in my department believe it is merely an engineering problem," the professor continued, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper. "They think we simply need thicker copper leads and larger resonators. But I believe they are blind. Like the great star-watchers of the ancient spires, I see a truth the academy refuses to acknowledge. Power is not a commodity mined from the dirt. It is the living resonance of connection between peoples and the land that shaped them." <!-- L1033 --> <!-- L1047 --> <!-- L1048 --> <!-- L1051 -->

The tortle lunged across the rug, grabbed an iron wrench, and slammed it onto the workbench. "The bureaucrats thought the Ash-Bloods would be easy," he scoffed. "'Fire is simple,' they declared. 'Heat is heat. Connect their forges, and the power will flow.' They understood nothing of the soul of the volcanic clans! And now they are baffled by the Mazizi. Have you spoken with the Mazizi cadets?" <!-- L1052 --> <!-- L1055 --> <!-- L1058 -->

"I heard them talking at the fire," Iggy said. "About death." <!-- L1065 -->

"Precisely!" the professor exclaimed, eyes alight with scholarly fervor. "To the Mazizi, death is not an ending; it is the vital hinge upon which all life turns. They die, yet they do not die. Our emissaries did not understand. In their foolish arrogance, the ministry shipped crates of heavy siege weapons to the border, thinking that if we helped the Mazizi conquer and clear their ancient jungle, a bond of brotherhood would form through violence! It was an unmitigated disaster. You cannot force harmonic connection by destroying the very soil that birthed a culture. You must understand how the environment shaped the spirit of the people, and bridge the divide through mutual comprehension. If you do not have the vocabulary for what Terra gives you, how can you ever share its power?" <!-- L1070 --> <!-- L1080 --> <!-- L1090 --> <!-- L1104 --> <!-- L1105 --> <!-- L1114 --> <!-- L1126 -->
