## THE VIEW FROM THE CRANE

"I'm not going to the room," Iggy announced. <!-- L0936 -->

Ignatius looked down at him. "Oh — where are you going?" <!-- L0937 -->

"I don't know." <!-- L0938 -->

"You're just not going to go to the dorm room?" <!-- L0939 -->

"No. There's so much to see." <!-- L0940 -->

"Yeah, but — we have class tomorrow." The words were out before Ignatius could stop them, and he heard himself say them with dawning horror. *Class tomorrow.* Thirty seconds of peer pressure and he had nearly become a person who went to bed on time. <!-- L0941 -->

"Look. Class." Iggy waved a small hand at the idea of class, dismissing the entire concept. "I need to find out things. I'm just gonna walk around. You don't have to come." <!-- L0942 --> <!-- L0944 -->

"Yeah, but now I'm curious." And Ignatius followed — not because the campus interested him half as much as this strange little creature did. He still wasn't one hundred percent sure what Iggy *was*. If Iggy was curious about everything else, Ignatius was curious about Iggy. Now was the time to figure out the person he was rooming with. <!-- L0945 --> <!-- L0952 -->

What Iggy was, it turned out, was a one-man geological survey. The journal came back out, along with a contraption from his bag of tricks — an enormous magnifying lens that amplified his already ginormous goggled eyes into something unsettling — and then he was down every hallway, examining everything, drawing everything. His notes, if you looked over his shoulder, resembled the scribblings of a beautiful mind in a thunderstorm: no system, no legend, no mercy. It made sense to him. <!-- L0959 -->

His pace was a natural disaster of a different kind — sudden wild-hair sprints ("Oh, this is good, this is good, this is good"), dead stops to thoroughly inspect something microscopic ("Oh. This is nothing"), constant muttering. He would turn without warning and press random objects into Ignatius's hands, pull new tools from the bag, inspect, stow, repeat. Chaos with a sampling methodology. Ignatius could make no sense of any of it, and followed anyway. <!-- L0965 -->

Outside, the campus presented Iggy with its final temptation: the construction cranes. He looked up at the nearest one, and something in him simply began to climb. <!-- L0979 -->

Then Loami arrived, drawn by the same plume-of-smoke logic, took one look up, and said to Aggie, "That looks promising. Come on." <!-- L0987 -->

Ignatius ended up at the base, holding Iggy's pack — because anything on that crane not bolted down was being unbolted and handed down. "Hey — take this one now." Piece by piece, the crane was being gently disassembled for science. <!-- L0988 -->

Higher and higher Iggy went, boots finding purchase, small hands hauling. He had been above the ground before — but never above the ground with *sky*. <!-- L0990 -->

"I think — at some point you can touch it, right?" he called down. "Like, I'm going to be able to touch it." <!-- L0995 -->

"I think you are touching it?" Ignatius called back. "What?" <!-- L0996 -->

"No, but like — I don't need to touch it. Never mind." <!-- L0999 --> <!-- L1001 -->

"I mean, we can get on the flying ship — it'll get us more in the air — but you *are* touching the air." Ignatius squinted up at him. "I didn't know you could feel it." <!-- L1002 -->

"But I don't feel it," Iggy said, with enormous seriousness. <!-- L1003 -->

"Come down, please." Britt's voice, arriving from below. She and Aggie had finished their lap of the campus and found, on the industrial side where the steam rose, a man on fire shouting up at a small creature hanging by its feet from a crane — and had followed the commotion the way you follow a plume of smoke. <!-- L1005 -->

"Well — I just need to see something." <!-- L1006 -->

"Who is that?" Britt asked Ignatius. <!-- L1010 -->

"Why are you guys following me?" Iggy called down. "Nobody ever follows me. I just need to see something." <!-- L1011 -->

"I'm pretty sure you're my roommate," Ignatius said. <!-- L1014 -->

"Come down." <!-- L1016 -->

"I feel fine," Ignatius offered, unhelpfully. <!-- L1023 -->

"Everything's fine, guys," Iggy said, dangling. "I just need to—" <!-- L1024 -->

Britt reached out, took hold of the scaffolding, and shook it. "Come down *now*." <!-- L1029 -->

"Hey, hey, hey, hey — what are you doing — okay! I'll come down. Hold on."

Iggy clung to the structure, notebook flapping open, still trying to balance and draw at once — because up here, he could *see it all*: the campus spread below him, and beyond it, on the horizon, steam towers and the distant crowns of balloons and masts and hulls — dozens of vessels, every shape and size imaginable, gathered offshore where they'd wait out the testing. <!-- L1032 --> <!-- L1038 -->

There was a cloud up there. One specific cloud. Iggy had never seen a cloud before — not from underneath open sky — and he was privately certain that if he climbed just a little higher, he could grab it. <!-- L1049 -->

And he was determined to get the whole thing onto one sheet: a map of where he was. <!-- L1054 -->

They were all gathered at the base of the crane — a man of soot and flame, a boiler-maker, a quiet woman and her chaos-sowing sister, and a small clay person descending at last with his notebook clutched to his chest — when a new voice lifted toward them. <!-- L1058 -->

"Hi! Um. What are y'all doing? Do you know each other? Oh my god." A small woman was approaching, prim and put-together, clutching a little notebook — dressed not in academy colors but with the unmistakable neatness of a very serious student of things. "Are you from the island? Are you — how would I know if you're — are you — are you *Ash-Bloods*? Three of you, all in one group! Oh, what a splendorous occasion." <!-- L1060 --> <!-- L1062 -->

"Take a photo," Britt said flatly. "It'll last longer." <!-- L1063 -->

Ignatius looked down at the woman, then sideways at the group, doing the math she had clearly already done — the Ember Isles didn't send delegates in threes. Whatever a splendorous occasion was, they were apparently it. <!-- L1066 -->
