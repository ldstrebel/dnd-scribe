## CHAPTER 49: PODCAST ARCHIVES & THE WASHINGTON LEAD

Naomi slapped a manila folder onto the grease-stained trestle table, scattering black-and-white printouts across the wood. She stood over the trio with arms crossed, her eyes burning with caffeinated intensity.

Pierre leaned back, nursing his tumbler of rum and orange juice. "All right, Naomi. Lay it on us." <!-- L1082 -->

"There is this podcast," Naomi began, tapping a printed transcript. "Two women who host a true-crime show—mostly they just get hammered and banter, but out of nowhere, this one episode has completely blown up online. It's about a commercial airliner flight headed into Washington, D.C. A man supposedly stood up in the aisle to hijack the plane. But before anyone could scream, he was found facedown in the carpet, bound with heavy zip-tie handcuffs. The plane landed safely at the airport, and zero passengers saw who tackled or cuffed him." <!-- L1083 -->

She leaned forward, her knuckles flat on the table. "No one saw what happened. No air marshal was on board. And right now, the internet is tearing itself apart trying to figure out who saved the flight." <!-- L1084 -->

Pierre adjusted his spectacles, swirling his drink. "Did Teddy fill you in on the master? The mastermind behind the Redactors?" <!-- L1085 -->

"I haven't really investigated that much," Naomi admitted, shaking her head. <!-- L1107 -->

"Teddy gave me a brief overview a long time ago when I was chasing fragments," she explained. "I've always just tracked down the physical artifacts. I haven't dug into where the mastermind is coming from, other than Teddy saying he felt the hand of this entity during his second fragment." <!-- L1108 -->

Pierre set his glass down with a deliberate clatter. "Here is something for your big brain to ponder. The creature in the cart told us her master is creating a utopia by altering small, random events—saving a handful of patients from a rogue physician, or sparing a single airliner. Why alter these minor incidents instead of preventing massive global horrors like the Holocaust? If you want to understand the mastermind, you must ask why he is remaking history into his twisted idea of egalitarian perfection." <!-- L1111 -->

Naomi stared at him, momentarily stunned. "Wow. I'm speechless. And that doesn't happen very often." <!-- L1113 -->

"Furthermore, one more thing," Pierre added, crossing his legs with delicate precision. "I do not believe we should go after any more fragments." <!-- L1115 -->

Naomi's eyes flared. "Why do you say that?" <!-- L1116 -->

"The master no longer cares about the fragments," Pierre argued, gesturing toward himself. "He cares about us! He knows the anomalies are absorbed inside us. If the villain says you are the most valuable target, maybe we need therapy first to understand what we have seen!" <!-- L1121 -->

"If we're important because we've absorbed fragments, wouldn't that mean we should collect more to become stronger?" Eusacles countered from across the bench. <!-- L1123 -->

"The doctor attacked as soon as we left the Margin," Pierre added anxiously. "I do not want to get attacked again." <!-- L1124 -->

"Teddy has been safe here for thirty or forty years without being attacked by a doctor," he pointed out. "It seems pretty safe to me. We could just never leave, and watch the movies Naomi bootlegs for us." <!-- L1128 -->

Alfie piped up from the tabletop, button eyes gleaming with sudden insight. "Is it like a Horcrux situation, like in Harry Potter? Where we are like Harry, in that we are important, but the fragments are also important?" <!-- L1130 -->

Pierre stared down at the tiny duelist in utter shock. "Alfie, did you stay up all night?" <!-- L1131 -->

"I've never seen the movies, but I have read all the books," Alfie admitted casually, smoothing his sailcloth lapels. "You never really asked me if I read books." <!-- L1134 -->

"Have you read comic books?" Pierre asked, bewildered. <!-- L1144 -->

"Yeah, all of them," Alfie nodded proudly. "Really, all I've got in my head is memories of sailors dying on ships, and a bunch of books and comics." <!-- L1153 -->

Alfie's small wooden arm twitched with thread consciousness, gesturing toward the folder. "What I'm trying to say is: are the fragments like Horcruxes? We are just unfortunate people put into a situation. Honestly, so was Harry Potter. I'm just an action figure who lived, you know." <!-- L1163 -->

Pierre covered his face with his hands, groaning in defeat. "Harry Potter had professors at an ancient castle teaching him real patronus charms, not a tiny white bunny that the monster ignores!" <!-- L1177 -->

"I do not feel good about walking through the Lost Roads expecting not to get ambushed by a woman sitting in the second row," he added. "We got lucky twice. Are we getting lucky a third time? It seems like too much." <!-- L1178 -->

"Don't they always say third time's the charm?" Alfie chirped cheerfully. "I think it's going to be great." <!-- L1183 -->

Eusacles drained his tumbler, looking directly at Naomi. "Are you suggesting there's another fragment over in D.C.?" <!-- L1188 -->

Naomi tapped a grainy photograph pinned to the last manila sheet—an evidence tag stamped with the seal of the Metropolitan Police Department. "Yes. The podcast confirmed that the flight manifest and the anomalous zip-tie handcuffs used to bind the hijacker are currently locked in an evidence locker at a police precinct in Washington, D.C." <!-- L1190 -->
