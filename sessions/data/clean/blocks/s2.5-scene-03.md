## CHAPTER 18: THE LONER & THE CRANE

The damp basalt masonry of the outer courtyard still radiated faint memories of the afternoon sun, but in the shadows beneath the construction scaffolding, the air was cool and smelled of dry loam and machine oil. Iggy sat tucked into the crevice between two foundation blocks, pulling the heavy collar of his woolen trench coat tight around his chin. Underneath his cap, the tiny sprigs of green moss and clover that crowned his packed-dirt brow bristled softly in the evening breeze. <!-- L0188 --> <!-- L0193 -->

For as long as he could remember, back in the subterranean gloom of the Trench, he had been the odd one out. His parents had sat on the elder council—dignified, stoic earth-kin who measured life in centuries of slow sediment and immovable duty. Iggy had never fit that mold. While the other youths learned the ritual chants and respected the ancient boundaries of the deep soil, Iggy had burrowed through forgotten tunnels, prying into restricted vaults until the day his calloused fingers unearthed the ancient copper-rimmed slate. <!-- L0195 --> <!-- L0198 --> <!-- L0201 -->

He had anticipated coming to the Academy as a ghost. A loner from the underworld who would slip through the lecture halls unremarked, take his measurements, and decipher the great machinery that made the surface spires hum. Instead, the universe seemed intent on playing a practical joke. <!-- L0206 --> <!-- L0210 --> <!-- L0215 -->

He had scored off the charts. The Loom slates had choked on his resonance test, gulping twice before yielding numbers that faculty whispered about in hushed, nervous corridors. And because he was small, odd, and bristling with moss, people had done the one thing he was completely unprepared for: they had begun gravitating toward him. <!-- L0216 --> <!-- L0222 --> <!-- L0228 --> <!-- L0235 -->

When he had climbed to the counterweight arm of the harbor crane earlier that afternoon, feet dangling hundreds of feet above the dark turquoise basin, Ignatius had followed him up without a moment's hesitation. The flame-haired youth had perched right beside him, talking as though they were old friends sharing a garden wall. Iggy had pretended to be annoyed, scowling behind his thick copper goggles, but beneath the packed-clay exterior, a strange, warm flutter had caught in his chest. <!-- L0238 --> <!-- L0240 --> <!-- L0244 -->

He had secretly enjoyed it. When the students down in the courtyard had shouted up, waving and inviting him down to the evening bonfire at Block 99, he had slid down that steel cable faster than dignity warranted. For the first time in his life, someone had asked him to belong. <!-- L0248 --> <!-- L0249 --> <!-- L0252 -->

Yet the old instincts were hard to quiet. A lifetime of isolation meant that whenever someone told him to stop tinkering or tried to herd him into a line, a prickly, stubborn barrier flared up. He wasn't trying to be difficult; he was simply unaccustomed to anyone caring what he did with his hands. <!-- L0257 --> <!-- L0261 --> <!-- L0266 -->

And ultimately, he had not traveled all the way from the dark deep of the Trench merely to make friends around a campfire. He had climbed that crane because he was desperately, relentlessly trying to understand the machines that powered this vast civilization. His home was struggling—strange pressure shifts and tremors had plagued the deep rock for seasons, threatening to collapse the ancient galleries. Even if the Trench elders had never truly understood him, even if they viewed his tinkering as foolish heresy, Iggy cared for his people with every grain of soil in his body. He would become whatever he needed to be—an unsung hero, a scavenger, a scholar of stolen cogs—if it meant finding the answers that could save his home. <!-- L0268 --> <!-- L0271 --> <!-- L0276 --> <!-- L0281 --> <!-- L0284 -->
