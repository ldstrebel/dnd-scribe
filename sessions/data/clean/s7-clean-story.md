## CHAPTER 42: THE UTOPIAN CART & THE PURPLE FLARE

The deafening howl of the campus fire klaxons had finally ceased, but the strobe lights still carved sharp amber arcs across the wet lawns. Distant sirens wailed toward the auditorium entrance, pulling emergency crews and the remaining stragglers away from the darkened perimeter. Half a quad away, tucked beneath the shadow of a concrete arcade where the temporal mist of the Lost Roads met modern West Virginia brick, Pierre, Alfie, and Eusacles shoved the gray rubberized janitor cart against a pillar.

Inside the deep vinyl bin, wedged among discarded rags and architectural trash, the bound figure stirred. The dark ink tendrils sprouting from the woman's scalp twitched, their oily sheen catching the flashing yellow emergency lights. As her swollen eyelids fluttered open, her gaze locked onto the trio with cold venom.

"You have no idea what we're capable of," she hissed, her voice vibrating with an unsettling, resonant rasp. <!-- L0214 -->

Eusacles crossed his denim-clad arms, his aviator sunglasses reflecting the distant flashing beacons. "Well, yeah. That's why we have you here. Obviously, we're going to ask you some questions because we have no idea what you're capable of. And that's where you come in, because you're gonna tell us." <!-- L0215 -->

"That's not going to happen," she snarled, straining against the heavy electrical cord binding her wrists behind her back. "You think you're just going to boss me around and tell me what to do? You fell to the ground like glass." <!-- L0216 -->

Her lip curled into a sneer as her black-veined eyes bore directly into Eusacles. "I was able to rip that from your mind." <!-- L0218 -->

"I mean, that's a good point," Eusacles conceded with an indifferent shrug, tapping the heavy head of his iron morningstar against his combat boot. "But now you're in a trash can. Anyway, all right, guys. She's being real resistant. Let's just kill her. That'll be way easier." <!-- L0219 -->

Pierre adjusted the brass bridge of his spectacles, lifting an elegant hand in protest. "Patience, Eusacles! We have not even begun to offer her choices. She has potential. We must discern what she was sent here to accomplish." <!-- L0220 -->

Eusacles grunted, sliding down against the damp brickwork to rest his battered shoulders. "I got to take a nap. I think I should rest." <!-- L0227 -->

"Take your rest, my friend," Pierre murmured, keeping his gaze locked on the prisoner as Alfie stood sentry on the cart's rim. Pierre leaned closer, his black beret tilted forward. "I am wondering... do you know who my mother is? It always sounds a little pedantic to ask, but every time I bring her up, people seem to clam up entirely." <!-- L0242 -->

The woman let out a wet, harsh cackle from the bottom of the bin. "I absolutely know who you are, and it's simply embarrassing that you have yet to find out what you truly are. You think you're simply a Gorgon? That's not even close." <!-- L0247 -->

Her smile turned cruel and mocking. "You're a joke. You're a prank." <!-- L0248 -->

Pierre blinked, a sudden crimson flush rising up his neck beneath his scarf. He turned toward the rim of the cart. "Alfie, would you mind slapping her for me?" <!-- L0249 -->

The two-foot driftwood duelist did not hesitate. Alfie dropped straight down onto the captive's chest with a hollow wooden thud, miniature boots planting firmly against her sternum. In a flash of polished steel, the curved hook at Alfie's wrist hovered barely an inch from the woman's left eyeball. "Now that's my friend you're talking to," Alfie growled, painted button eyes narrowing. "You watch what you say." <!-- L0264 -->

The woman spat a fleck of dark blood against Alfie's sailcloth tunic, entirely unimpressed by the tiny blade. "And you expect me to listen to you? You're junk." <!-- L0266 -->

"junk." <!-- L0270 -->

Alfie's small wooden jaw set like iron. "We don't say the D word around here. We don't say it. And because you said it, I think you owe me." With a sudden, surgical hiss, Alfie's needle rapier lashed out. A three-inch severed tendril tumbled free, spurting thick black ichor across the plastic rim. "That one's free. Next time you insult me or one of my friends, it's going to be two." <!-- L0273 -->

The woman shrieked in agony as the stump pulsed violently, the dark fluid smoking where it struck the linoleum. ## CHAPTER 43: THE SIX-FINGERED SHADOW & THE PATRONUS FLIGHT

The blinding purple flare arced high over the university rooftops, bathing the brick arches and trimmed quad lawns in an unnatural violet glare before vanishing into the night. An oppressive silence crashed down over the breezeway. The janitor cart sat motionless in the gloom, its plastic rim slick with cooling black ichor.

"Time to go," Pierre whispered, his voice trembling as he backed away from the cart. <!-- L0354 -->

Eusacles adjusted his grip on his morningstar, his knuckles white. "We should leave, right? Like one hundred percent." <!-- L0357 -->

Alfie leapt lightly from the lip of the bin, landing squarely on the shoulder of Eusacles' heavy denim jacket. "Time to go." <!-- L0361 -->

Pierre caught Professor Dravin by the elbow, guiding the rumpled academic swiftly toward the shadows along the perimeter. Dravin moved in a daze, clutching his tweed coat around himself as the cold autumn wind whipped through the colonnade. Eusacles fell into stride beside them, head turning constantly. "Leave. We're leaving now." <!-- L0366 -->

Eusacles' dark aviators caught a flicker of movement across the wide lawn. Beneath the amber halo of a distant security floodlight, something crawled through the gloom. It possessed no feet, drifting inches above the manicured grass like a pool of living calligraphy. Where its hands emerged from its ragged ink cloak, six elongated fingers flexed in twitching rhythm. Its head was a blank oval void, yet beneath the translucent surface of the skin, dozens of drowned, silent faces shifted and strained against each other in endless torment. The thing was sniffing the air, tracking the ionized trail left by the purple flare.

"We got to get out of here," Eusacles muttered through clenched teeth, his voice dead flat with urgent dread. "I will explain when we're gone, but we're getting out of here now. And we can't be followed." <!-- L0376 -->

"Alfie, can you ride on my shoulder so you can lead me, so I don't get lost a third time?" he asked as they broke into a disciplined jog toward the tree line. <!-- L0390 -->

"Yeah, all right, mate," Alfie replied, anchoring miniature driftwood boots into the denim seam. <!-- L0393 -->

As they slipped between the weeping willows toward the threshold seam where reality thinned into mist, Alfie pulled out a scrap of cotton waste, scrubbing furiously at the needle rapier. "This won't come off. She's really gross." <!-- L0408 -->

"Yeah," Alfie muttered, sheathing the stained needle. "The ink was a bit much." <!-- L0414 -->

"Yeah, that was a bit much," Eusacles agreed, his gaze fixed on the thickening fog ahead. "Hey, while we're on this road and walking... the thing that I saw back there. "No facial features, but lots of faces inside the void," Eusacles continued, his voice tight. "Almost as if something was trapped within a body and didn't know what it was. I was horrified." <!-- L0431 -->

Alfie shivered on his shoulder. "Yeah, that's not for me." <!-- L0433 -->

"Not my type either," Pierre murmured. <!-- L0435 -->

Across the lawn, fifty paces away, the six-fingered shape had reached the janitor cart. It drifted above the blood-soaked rim, its blank oval head tilting as its many submerged faces swirled. Slowly, deliberately, the creature turned toward the tree line, its hollow visage locking onto Pierre.

## CHAPTER 45: RETURN TO THE MARGIN & THE MENDED SPATULA

The morning sun filtered through the canopy of ancient oaks as the party guided Susanna down the final gravel switchback toward the clearing of the Margin. The sanctuary cabins rested peacefully against the forested ridge, woodsmoke curling lazily from the central stone chimney.

Susanna walked close beside Pierre, her hands tucked into the oversized sleeves of Dravin's spare tweed jacket.
