In the deep canopy of the southern coast, the world was never meant to last forever. <!-- L0004 -->

For centuries uncounted, the dense petrified megaflora had isolated the south from the northern empire of Harmony. The Mizizi lived within the hollows of ancient, stone-thick boles rising like fossilized titans from the damp loam, their subterranean dwellings burrowed beneath living amber and sprawling fungal shelves. They were a peaceful folk of bark, shell, and mycelium, their skin carrying the earthy texture of beings born from damp soil. <!-- L0032 --> <!-- L0040 -->

Their elders lived by an ancient, harrowing covenant: *when things stop dying, we start dying.*

To the outside world, death was an enemy to be delayed by medicine and machine. To the turtle-mushroom folk of the canopy, decomposition was the breath of life itself. The great rot fed the soil, the soil nourished the roots, and the spore-networks bound the living to the dead in an unbroken circle of renewal. The dense petrified jungle had always stood as a living barrier, keeping the world apart. In the clan's oldest lore, the warning ran deep: if outsiders ever breached the frontier, the sacred decay would cease. <!-- L0042 --> <!-- L0044 --> <!-- L0048 -->

And two cycles ago, the rot had stalled. <!-- L0050 -->

Wood that should have softened into fertile loam under the rains remained dry and stubborn. Fungi withered without releasing spores. Panic rippled through the root-halls. Then, along the southern beaches, the mists parted as two rival exploranauts from the northern empire breached the frontier in a desperate race: Captain Elara Thorne veering east toward the jagged volcanic peaks, and Valentine Sterling Sr. steering south into the petrified bay, each betting these colossal landmarks held sentient life if anywhere would. <!-- L0052 --> <!-- L0056 -->

Sterling stepped ashore into the root-hall — immaculate, cold, and calculating, dressed in a spotless uniform devoid of dirt, moss, or holes. The elders called him the Paper Man. But Harmony had not come to conquer. Sterling came seeking connection, politely asking what the clan desired in trade.

To his astonishment, the Mizizi did not ask for medicines, food, or textiles. They asked for weapons. Not to wage war against the newcomers, but to turn against their own land. Desperate to restart the sacred rot before their people perished, the elders took the heavy steel axes and saws into the woods, frantically hacking into their own ancestral trees to force the wood into decay. <!-- L0058 --> <!-- L0062 --> <!-- L0073 -->

Shortly after first contact, an ancient rite occurred: the Exchange. An outsider, a brilliant and fierce river-kin named Rill, arrived and joined the clan. But Rill quickly clashed with the conservative elders over their futile tree-hacking, storming off with the departing traders to discover what was truly happening across the sea. Two young cousins, Britt and Aggie, watched her leave from beneath a shelf-mushroom arch, the memory burning deep into their minds. <!-- L0078 --> <!-- L0084 --> <!-- L0090 --> <!-- L0093 -->

Miles beneath the surface loam, far below the reach of sun and sky, rested an ancient stone metropolis that had never heard the name of Harmony. <!-- L0115 -->

The Trench-Kin were beings of compressed shale, packed dirt, and damp clay. Their kingdom was an abyssal labyrinth of carved basalt masonry and cavernous chambers submerged beneath colossal water columns, where hydrostatic pressure was heavy and comforting like an iron blanket. For centuries, they had lived entirely self-contained, measuring time by tectonic shifts and subterranean currents. <!-- L0128 --> <!-- L0130 -->

To a soil-kin, the surface was not a frontier; it was a hostile, desiccating void where the lack of pressure made limbs feel dangerously brittle. <!-- L0134 -->

Iggy had never belonged to the quiet routine of the deep warrens. Small even for a dirt-kin, with round copper goggles strapped over his eyes, he was driven by an insatiable curiosity that the burrow-masters called madness. He took apart water pumps, collected glowing thermal mosses, and filled scrap parchment with erratic engineering diagrams. <!-- L0149 -->

The only formal contact the subterranean folk had maintained with the surface was the sacred River Exchange. Every few seasons, young candidates drew lots and were sent blindfolded up to the sunlit riverbank, never to return, while surface river-folk were welcomed down into the deep to refresh their bloodlines. It was a solemn, necessary duty. <!-- L0151 --> <!-- L0169 --> <!-- L0171 --> <!-- L0173 -->

Lately, the deepkin had noticed the air growing thin and stifling in the lowest tunnels. But recently, the currents had shifted, and breathing had become strangely easier.

Then came the day of the expected exchange.

No blindfolded traveler walked down the riverbank. Instead, an engraved stone slab was dropped through the water column into the deep grotto, bearing a crisp imperial message: the Exchange was no longer necessary. Above the waves, a colossal vessel — a hull as vast as a leviathan, yet resting impossibly upon the surface instead of in the depths — had dropped anchor in the basin.

Iggy found the engraved stone resting in his parents' dwelling. His curiosity was ignited. While the warrens slept, he followed the subterranean thermal conduits upward, slipping past the granite threshold into the sunlit current above. <!-- L0177 -->

Across the eastern straits, rising like a jagged black crown against the sea mist, lay the Ash-Blood Isles. <!-- L0200 -->

The Ash-Bloods were a people born of basalt and magma, their terraced streets clinging to the inner cliffs of a colossal volcanic caldera. Ancient legends held that their ancestors had been half-obsidian giants whose veins burned with primal fire. <!-- L0202 --> <!-- L0204 -->

Yet for generations, the great caldera had been dying. The fiery lakes had hardened into black glass; geysers blew cold mud; and the clan lived in terror that their ancestral hearth would freeze over completely. <!-- L0208 --> <!-- L0210 -->

Then Captain Elara Thorne sailed into the bay. <!-- L0212 -->

Unlike the aloof merchants of the southern woods, Elara pitched her tent directly inside the freezing caldera, sharing meals and forming a profound personal bond with Matriarch Lady Ignis. To demonstrate Harmony's science, Elara set up a portable cooking grill powered by a bronze **resonator** — a delicate frequency tuner that channeled harmonic vibration to sear food in the freezing wind. In return, Lady Ignis chiseled a piece of dormant lavsidian stone and sent it across the sea to cook an imperial feast in Harmony Prime.

The moment the connection across the ocean closed, the ancient resonance sparked.

Deep within the caldera, the black glass shattered with a roar. Torrents of crimson magma detonated from the vents, surging back to life and sending Elara scrambling up the cliffs in breathless laughter. The hearth was rekindled.

Lady Ignis prepared to depart for Harmony Prime to take her sovereign seat under the Inverse Power Doctrine and sign the Expansion Bill authorizing the relocation of Vumbua Academy across the sea. With her went the clan's first wave of brightest prospects — including Ember, Ignatius's talented cousin and quasi-rival, selected to attend the prestigious Academy in the capital. Elara pulled Lady Ignis aside, offering a quiet warning of the explosive political power and public celebrity awaiting her across the ocean.

Watching from the caldera rim with soot on his hands was young Ignatius. He had been judged too reckless, not quite ready to make the first leap across the sea. <!-- L0221 --> <!-- L0223 -->

Ignatius looked down at the departing steam launches with burning pride and stubborn resolve. "Next year," he murmured, his hair sparking with determined embers. "If that academy comes to our shore... I will be there." <!-- L0227 --> <!-- L0231 --> <!-- L0235 --> <!-- L0242 -->

In the steaming mechanical bowels of the Academy citadel, nobody cared about ancient prophecies. They cared about steam pressure. <!-- L0260 -->

Loami was a broad-shouldered boilermaker whose bloodline was written in grease, coal dust, and calluses. For generations uncounted, his family had stoked the furnaces and turned the wrenches that kept Harmony moving forward. His creed was simple: civilization did not run on aristocratic speeches. It ran on pressure valves, fireboxes, and working hands in the dark. <!-- L0269 --> <!-- L0271 --> <!-- L0274 -->

For eighty years, the northern empire had grown stagnant. On the old continent, the Resonance Races — Harmony’s premier national sport — had grown boring and predictable on flat open-ocean courses, losing viewership. The maps had yellowed. The frontier had stalled. <!-- L0276 --> <!-- L0278 --> <!-- L0306 --> <!-- L0308 --> <!-- L0312 -->

Then came the telegraphs of the twin clan discoveries.

The news detonated across Harmony like wildfire. Public excitement reached a fever pitch for a daring new raceway to be carved directly into the vertical basalt cliffs of the southern continent: **The Apex Arena**.

When the clearance was signed to unmoor Vumbua Academy and relocate it south, Loami was in the boiler room as part of the vanguard fleet. The migration was a colossal naval convoy: the titanic Deep-Hull steamship and soaring Sky-Spires steamed across the churning swells, escorted by ironclads firing heavy guns into the fog to drive off prowling aether beasts. Loami was the first of the new wave, bolting down scaffolding and securing the massive cranes that would raise the new city over the southern basin. <!-- L0282 --> <!-- L0284 --> <!-- L0292 --> <!-- L0299 --> <!-- L0302 --> <!-- L0314 --> <!-- L0317 --> <!-- L0319 -->

A year later, at the edge of the petrified forest, the traders returned. <!-- L0344 -->
