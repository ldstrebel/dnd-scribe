## CHAPTER 30: THE CAMPUS AT UNIVERSITY UNIVERSITY & THE TIN-FOIL PROTEST

Pierre balanced the flat bronze tip of his newly acquired museum javelin over the iron grate, deftly flipping another thin crepe as the hearth flared. The dark rum from Mike's private reserve sizzled violently against the hot metal, filling the low-ceilinged room with a rich, caramelized vapor. <!-- L0407-L0409 -->

Eusacles eyed the skillet with famished desperation, wiping grease from his scarred chin. "Does the Frenchman actually know what he's doing with that spear, or am I about to eat metal shavings?" <!-- L0411-L0413:eusacles -->

"I have practiced this culinary discipline for three straight days, Monsieur Gambler," Pierre sniffed, tossing his chin indignantly as his woolen beret slid toward his ear. He expertly slid the folded pastry onto a tin plate with the javelin point. "Mike’s secret dark rum gives it an exquisite Parisian depth. You are full of crude American spice—this sweetness will civilize your palate." <!-- L0414-L0419:pierre -->

Eusacles took a massive, unceremonious bite, his eyebrows shooting up above his aviator frames. "Damn. That is a straight-up pirate crepe." <!-- L0420:eusacles -->

At the far edge of the pine bench, away from the hearthsmoke, Alfie sat journaling. Her thoughts drifted back through their perilous journey—back to the library basement when Professor Dravin had cast *Sleep* against their ambushers, and Alfie's latent Wordcraft had flared instinctually for the first time, mutating the incantation into *Sheep* and leaving the room echoing with bewildered bleats. She hadn't even known her driftwood frame possessed such phonetic power then. Ever since, between night watches at the Margin, she had studied fragments of arcana, realizing she could anchor a permanent Wordcraft inscription right into her seasoned timber like a sailor's tattoo. One reliable alteration, ready to trigger without draining her spark. <!-- L0422 -->

Suddenly, an electric tremor seized the grain of her forearm, pinching like an exposed wire. Her fingers snapped open, closing around her miniature whittling knife with uncanny speed. The steel hovered over her seasoned wood, humming with latent arcane resonance. <!-- L0424-L0426 -->

"What do you reckon would be most useful, mates?" Alfie asked, knife poised above her wood as she looked across the table. <!-- L0429:alfie -->

Eusacles glanced up from his plate, leaning over the table. "What are you doing with that whittling blade, little one?" <!-- L0435:eusacles -->

Pierre adjusted his spectacles, his eyes lighting up with speculative mischief. "I like the idea of Alfie with a Chaos Belt. Does it summon random French baguettes, or something considerably more violent?" <!-- L0442:pierre -->

Dravin set down his teacup, nodding thoughtfully. "Classical historiography records several threshold talismans. A matrix of unpredictable tools—some miraculous, others disastrously absurd. A Chaos Belt could be remarkably potent." <!-- L0444:dravin -->

"I think we got to go Chaos Belt," Alfie decided, a sharp grin carving across her wooden features. "Changed *Chaos Bolt* to *Chaos Belt*. Carved right into my timber so I don't burn an honest reserve of spark when things go sideways. I picture reaching down in a brawl, pulling out whatever strange device the loom feels like tossing me." <!-- L0446-L0478:alfie -->

With swift, biting strokes, her needle knife bit into the grain of her forearm, shaving away curls of gray driftwood. Shimmering amber light welled from the fresh gouges, etching the sharp runic letters *CHAOS BELT* deep into her bark, glowing with vibrant empowerment. <!-- L0487 -->

"An admirable adaptation, Alfie," Dravin concluded with quiet scholarly satisfaction, tapping his leather notebook. <!-- L0500-L0514:dravin -->

Heavy footsteps creaked on the floorboards as Theodore approached the common table. The burly surveyor carried a rolled canvas terrain map, resting his calloused hands on the back of Dravin’s chair. <!-- L0528 -->

"Before you folks set off for the college, you have a logistical choice to make," Theodore said in a low, measured rumble. "Young Hermes has his own delivery network—the Hermes Express, he calls it. If you pay his price, he can fold space through the postal corridors and drop you directly on the campus steps. Eusacles has been helpful walking the camp perimeter out in the woods alone, but most of you have never used his dispatch. The alternative is the Forgotten Trail. It runs northeast through the untamed foothills. It is overgrown and wild, but it keeps you entirely off the celestial registry." <!-- L0529-L0537:theodore -->

Pierre waved his fork dismissively, his lip curling in haughty disdain. "I also deeply doubt the integrity of a so-called divine messenger who does not believe France is a real nation! Does he only do continental United States shipping? It is extraordinarily rude! What possible value are we to derive from a courier who thinks Europe is an imaginary prank? No, I am ready to speak with these scholars. Let us take the Forgotten Trail!" <!-- L0538:pierre -->
