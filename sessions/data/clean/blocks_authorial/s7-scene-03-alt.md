## CHAPTER 44: THE LOST ROADS & THE RESCUE OF SUSANNA

The damp, muffled silence of the Lost Roads settled over the party like a thick woolen blanket. The world here existed in perpetual twilight, bounded by gnarled elder trees and ancient stone trail markers etched with languages long erased from mortal memory. Professor Dravin walked between them in weary silence, his hand steady on his pocket watch as Eusacles led the way along the mossy flagstones.

A sudden crash of snapping branches broke the quiet.

From a dense thicket of pale ferns, a disheveled figure stumbled out onto the path, nearly colliding with Eusacles' chest. Pierre gasped, his spectacles nearly sliding down his nose as he recognized the woman immediately. It was Susanna—the young museum attendant from the North Carolina Museum of Natural History whom Pierre had shielded from the Gorgon gaze weeks ago before directing her into Theodore's maintenance shed. <!-- L0456 -->

Susanna stumbled backward, her wide brown eyes darting between their familiar faces and the swirling temporal mists behind them. "What? Where did you come from? Where am I? What is going on?" <!-- L0464:susanna -->

Pierre stepped forward, arms extended in theatrical greeting. "Oh, Susanna! Do not tell me you have been stuck in the Lost Roads for two weeks now! What have you eaten? You are coming with us. Come on!" <!-- L0465:pierre -->

Susanna pressed her hands to her temples, trembling as the chill vapor swirled around her ankles. "I don't even know how long it's been," she breathed, her voice cracking with exhaustion. "It seems like it's been months, but also just a few hours. This place is really confusing. I don't know where I'm going, but somehow it feels familiar." <!-- L0470:susanna -->

Pierre leaned against a mossy boulder with practiced Parisian poise, tipping his beret with a roguish smile. "I have to say, Susanna, you are holding together much better than I would have in a formless, timeless purgatory. You're sure you're not between Heaven and Hell or something like that?" <!-- L0471:pierre -->

He paused, lowering his tone to a melodic murmur. "Because you know that would make me your angel." <!-- L0474:pierre -->

Eusacles coughed into his fist, turning aside with an incredulous grin. "That was smooth as fuck." <!-- L0477:eusacles -->

Alfie piped up from his shoulder, nodding sagely. "Yeah, that's our boy." <!-- L0478:alfie -->

"She's looking pretty good for someone who was turning into a rock," Eusacles noted, looking Susanna up and down. <!-- L0488:eusacles -->

"Hey, easy," Pierre scolded playfully, waving them off. "I am talking to Susanna here!" <!-- L0492:pierre -->

Alfie ignored Pierre, pointing a miniature wooden thumb up toward the battered blue souvenir baseball cap perched over his painted brow. "Hey, Susanna, check it out!" <!-- L0496:alfie -->

Susanna blinked at the tiny driftwood figure, then at the North Carolina Museum of Natural History logo stitched into the faded cap. A faint, disbelieving laugh escaped her lips. "I guess I am not dead... unless they made the museum in purgatory." <!-- L0501:susanna -->

"No, you're not dead," Eusacles confirmed, rolling his shoulders and scanning the surrounding mist. "Does it feel good? You're not dead." <!-- L0508:eusacles -->

Alfie glanced down at Eusacles with a sly wink, tapping his tiny needle against his belt. As their gazes met, the heavy fog around the trail seemed to thin, revealing thick, sprawling brambles lining the mossy verge that none of them had noticed before. The thorny bushes hung heavy under clusters of fat, midnight-blue berries and golden wild plums, glistening with fragrant dew.

"Hell yeah," Eusacles muttered, grinning as he reached out to pluck a handful. "Look at these berries." <!-- L0528:eusacles -->

Susanna looked back at Pierre, a soft flush warming her pale cheeks as she caught his dazzling, boyish smile beneath the silver mist. "Yeah... I guess you are my angel. I am so glad to see you, and the rest of you, of course." <!-- L0539:susanna -->

Pierre beamed, preening as he smoothed the lapels of his rumpled coat. "Do you think we have time for a picnic? Maybe pick some berries for jam for the pancakes?" <!-- L0564:pierre -->

"Yeah," Alfie agreed, already leaning down from Eusacles' shoulder to snatch a ripe cluster of berries. "We always got time for berries." <!-- L0567:alfie -->

Together, the party harvested the sweet, wild fruit, filling their pockets and Dravin's canvas satchel as they continued down the winding stone track. The winding path gradually sloped downward, cutting through a high rhododendron thicket before spilling out onto a familiar sunlit rise.

The pearlescent fog dissipated in the crisp mountain air. Below them lay the gravel driveway, the split-rail cedar fences, and the weathered wood-shingled roof of the main cabin at the Margin. The pale golden light of early dawn was just cresting the eastern ridge, turning the morning frost to glittering diamonds.

"And it's daytime," Eusacles grunted, blinking against the sudden sunlight as they descended the grassy slope toward the sanctuary porch. <!-- L0574:eusacles -->
