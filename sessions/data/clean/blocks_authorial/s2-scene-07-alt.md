Inside the back room of the timber lodge, red string and pinned photographs spanned across Naomi's sprawling conspiracy board, linking geographic sightings of cosmic anomalies across the Eastern seaboard. Dravin leaned against a rough cedar bookshelf, his mind turning over the theoretical mechanics of their displacement—how historical anomalies echoed through time like revolutionary revisions to an ancient manuscript. <!-- L1161 -->

Theodore stood beside the corkboard, resting his calloused palms against the timber table as he addressed the newcomers. "I guess you have time now to come hear about what's going on—about why you're really here." <!-- L1177 -->

Pierre entered balancing a warm platter of flatbread, but paused when he noticed the ancient tomes resting beside his traveling pack. Dravin adjusted his wire rims, his scholarly gaze narrowing upon the weathered vellum bindings. "You took books from the Library of the Fates," Dravin observed with dry wonder. <!-- L1185 -->

Pierre set down the platter with a theatrical shrug, pulling three ancient scrolls from the leather folds. "These were on the shelves of the Fates. Naomi said they might guide our path." <!-- L1193 -->

Pierre tapped the Greek lettering inscribed along the margins of the aged sheepskin. "Yeah, I wrote down that one, and then there was that other..." <!-- L1209 -->

During their frantic flight through the collapsing planar stacks, Pierre had also scavenged geological charts and archaic volcanic texts that had inexplicably called to him, searching for clues to his monstrous heritage. <!-- L1213 -->

Naomi leaned over the worktable, carefully unrolling the oldest parchment. With Dravin assisting in deciphering the archaic dialect, a distinct passage stood out—a direct reference to the *Lost Roads*, the decommissioned metaphysical bypasses that linked forgotten settlements across the continent. <!-- L1233 -->

Beside the parchment lay the dog-eared pamphlet Pierre had studied before bed: *The Dummy's Guide to Gorgons*. <!-- L1236 -->

The maternal figure who had raised Pierre in the French countryside was not recorded among the classical dynasties, but the ancestral lineage trickled steadily upward through forgotten centuries. <!-- L1240 -->

Theodore nodded solemnly, his weathered index finger tracing the ink across the parchment. "Every ancient line traces back to the beginning. It all leads back to Echidna—the primordial Mother of Monsters." <!-- L1249 -->

Pierre pointed to the translated notes in his lap, connecting the pieces with rising excitement. "You're saying that this book that we have translated has a passage about the Lost Roads?" <!-- L1256 -->

Eusacles pushed off the cedar wall, rolling his broad shoulders with practical spartan resolve. "Never mind, I'll go." <!-- L1265 -->

Theodore watched the party rally around the expedition, a rare flicker of hope crossing his lined face. "I have never heard of anyone reaching three fragments before. Bring it home safely." <!-- L1269 -->
