## CHAPTER 47: CAMPFIRE WHISPERS & THE ENGRAVED WATCH

The afternoon shadows lengthened across the Margin, stretching toward the central fire pit where the ashes of last night's blaze smoldered. Teddy leaned on his cane, listening intently as Pierre and Eusacles recounted the campus ambush.

"Susanna said she ran into people who were inked up," Eusacles noted, tossing a cedar split onto the embers. "And that crazy tentacle-haired lady said something about us being heroes. I just want to make one thing clear: we are not heroes. I know what happens in every Greek tragedy when heroes get involved. We are not heroes." <!-- L0874:eusacles -->

Pierre nodded emphatically from the bench, brushing lint from his woolen beret. "Everyone I ever knew who acted like a hero ended up as a statue in my backyard. So I would agree—that is not a good definition." <!-- L0875:pierre -->

Teddy gave a somber nod. "I see the sentiment, and I agree. Let's not refer to ourselves as heroes. That does tend to lead to bad things overall." <!-- L0876:teddy -->

As night fell over the valley, the sanctuary grew quiet. The crickets chattered softly against the chill mountain air, and the residents retreated to their cabins. Eusacles made his way across the dark meadow to his own rough-hewn shelter on the perimeter, pulling his hammock tight between two sturdy cedar posts.

Sometime after midnight, a sudden, chill draft whistled through the chinks in the timber walls.

The air in the cabin turned crystalline and breathless. A soft, whispering flutter brushed past Eusacles' temple—the scraping caress of a large black feather sweeping across his brow. A flash of pale moonlight flared behind his closed eyelids, followed by deep silence.

Eusacles opened his eyes.

He was still in his shelter, yet the room felt impossibly vast and luminous, washed in an ethereal silver radiance. Sitting casually on the foot of the rough bench was a tall, lean figure cloaked in shadows, dark feathered wings folded neatly behind broad shoulders.

"Oh man," the figure said, his voice deep, soothing, and resonant like distant thunder rolling over calm waters. "That was a pretty tough one, huh? Got you down there for a second. Seemed like that didn't go super well." <!-- L0895:thanatos -->

Eusacles sat up in his hammock, blinking through the gloom. A crooked smirk tugged at the corner of his mouth. "Hey, Pops. Nice of you to pop in. It's only been what—how many years ago did you leave Mom? It's fine." <!-- L0896:eusacles -->

Thanatos, the Greek god of peaceful death, gave an apologetic grimace, adjusting the dark mantle over his collar. "It's complicated." <!-- L0897:thanatos -->

"Isn't everything complicated in the god standard?" Eusacles retorted dryly, rubbing his bruised ribs. "Yeah, that sucked. That wasn't great." <!-- L0900:eusacles -->

"I'm sorry I wasn't there in the moment," Thanatos said softly, his silver eyes glowing faintly in the dim room. "I'm just glad you knew, and that you were able to understand that this was not your time to go. The clock had not struck twelve, as we like to say." <!-- L0901:thanatos -->

"Well, if anyone would know, it'd be you, I guess," Eusacles muttered. <!-- L0902:eusacles -->

"Exactly." <!-- L0903:thanatos -->

Eusacles swung his legs out of the hammock, leaning forward with his elbows on his knees. "So, in fear of ending up on some horrible quest, what is going on? Do you have any idea what's happening? Because we're trying to piece together this whole story. We got a talking action figure, a French guy, and no one knows who their parents are. I haven't seen you in years. Clearly, shit is going on." <!-- L0904:eusacles -->

Thanatos sighed, resting his chin on his palm. "It's tough to explain, and we don't really know. The gods don't necessarily know. It's sort of a gray area. What has been going on for a few hundred years—which is basically a blink to us—is kind of an extradition situation. It's in the Fates' department. It's not really Olympus or Hades. It's an independent third-party paperwork department. We're about the action, and they take the notes." <!-- L0911:thanatos -->

Eusacles stared at him. "Can you do me one favor? Just one favor. I never ask you for anything. You've missed a lot of Christmases and birthdays. Can a man get just a little bit of armor? Just a little bit. I got hit way harder than I thought, and I don't want to see you when I'm not supposed to see you, if you get my gist." <!-- L0944:eusacles -->

Thanatos tilted his dark head, a faint smile touching his lips. "Do you still have the watch?" <!-- L0947:thanatos -->

"I still got the watch," Eusacles said, unhooking the heavy bronze timepiece from his vest. "Turns into my morningstar." <!-- L0948:eusacles -->

Thanatos reached out and took the pocket watch into his pale hands. With a slender, obsidian-tipped fingernail, the death god traced a slow, glowing sigil into the tarnished metal backing. Sparks of violet and silver light flared along the casing before sinking deep into the clockwork gears.

Thanatos handed it back. "Check the time. It's not yours. Consider this your tier 2 birthright: whenever you are below a quarter of your strength, your skin hardens with the chill of the grave, granting you two additional paces of warding armor. A little charm, a bit of my power onto you to hopefully prevent you from seeing me too early, and prevent me from looking like an idiot." <!-- L0949:thanatos -->

"Oh, that's kind of sick," Eusacles admitted, weighing the newly blessed watch in his palm. "I do appreciate that." <!-- L0950:eusacles -->

He paused, glancing up at his father through narrowed eyes. "Hey... do you know who Pierre's mom is?" <!-- L0955:eusacles -->

Thanatos froze. His dark brow arched, and then, slowly, a look of profound, incredulous amusement spread across his divine visage. "Pierre... that's the French guy, right?" <!-- L0956:thanatos -->

"Yeah, that's the guy who makes really good pancakes," Eusacles said. <!-- L0960:eusacles -->

Thanatos threw his head back and burst into rich, booming laughter, slapping his knee in delight. "I can't believe he's real! This is hilarious! What an epic prank! Oh man... did Hermes see him?" <!-- L0964:thanatos -->

"Yeah," Eusacles said, bewildered. "Hermes came by." <!-- L0971:eusacles -->

"Oh, I am shocked that he hasn't let that spill!" Thanatos wheezed, wiping a tear of divine mirth from his eye. "My goodness. Epic. Truly epic." <!-- L0973:thanatos -->

Eusacles glared at him. "You're not going to tell me, are you?" <!-- L0974:eusacles -->

"No, that would ruin the fun!" Thanatos grinned, standing tall and shaking his dark head. "I can't take credit for this." <!-- L0977:thanatos -->

"You are so fucking petty," Eusacles grunted. "All right. Well, Dad... I hate to admit it, but it was good to see you." <!-- L0978:eusacles -->

"Good to see you too, son," Thanatos murmured warmly. His great feathered wings swept around him in a swirl of midnight velvet, collapsing inward until a sleek black bat fluttered upward from the floorboards, squeaking once before darting through the high gable window into the night sky. <!-- L0983:thanatos -->
