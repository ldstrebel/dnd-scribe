The air conditioning inside the interstate transit bus had surrendered somewhere past Henderson, leaving the cabin to bake in the flat, white furnace of the Nevada desert. <!-- L0958 -->

Heat shimmered off the cracked asphalt of Highway 15, warping the barren Mojave horizon into a watery mirage. Inside, the stagnant air smelled of scorched vinyl, diesel exhaust, and overheated engine belts. The bus rattled violently with every dip in the road, its worn suspension squealing in metallic agony while the metal luggage racks groaned overhead. <!-- L0959 -->

Near the back, Pierre pressed himself flat against the cracked vinyl seat. Sweat trickled down his temple, itching against the woolen band of the beret he refused to take off. His knuckles clamped around the frayed straps of his canvas rucksack, hugging the bag tight against his chest. America was vast, mercilessly bright, and far too hot. He reached up, his fingertips nervously checking the edge of the woolen cap to ensure every stray, restless curl remained tucked out of sight. To any passerby, he had to look like an ordinary foreign exchange student—a quiet kid with glasses, an unassuming beret, and a bag full of coursework. Nothing more.

A few rows ahead, Professor Edward Dravin sat hunched forward in a rumpled tweed coat completely unsuited for triple-digit heat. Wire-rimmed spectacles perched precariously on the bridge of his nose as he turned the dog-eared pages of a monograph on classical Greek antiquities. His salt-and-pepper hair caught the dust motes dancing in the sunbeams, his pencil scratching rapid annotations along the page edges, oblivious to the groaning chassis beneath his feet.

Across the narrow aisle, Eusacles sprawled across two bench seats, his thick shoulders straining the seams of a fitted black tank top. His heavy black wristwatch caught the glare of the desert sun as he tapped the dial with an impatient thumb. Las Vegas was supposed to be a weekend of dice, neon, and cheap drinks—a clean escape from the persistent shadows of half-blood lineage he had spent years trying to ignore. He glared toward the rattling ceiling vents, unimpressed by the heat and thoroughly exhausted by the silence of the two academics sharing the cabin.

Suspended from the low ceiling at the front of the aisle, a boxy CRT television flickered through heavy static, droning out a tinny midday broadcast. <!-- L0960 -->

"...sweltering heat and high desert winds continue to break records across the Southwest," the news anchor's monotone voice buzzed through the speaker. "In national news, authorities confirm that the commercial flight bound for the capital has landed safely outside D.C. We turn now to our field reporter—" <!-- L0961 -->

A deafening screech tore through the cabin as the rear axle shuddered violently. <!-- L0962-L0963 -->

Slam. The floor twisted sideways, hurling luggage from the racks. <!-- L0964-L0965 -->

Slam. Centrifugal force dissolved into zero gravity, pitching passengers upward in a weightless spin. <!-- L0966-L0967 -->

A blinding detonation of searing white light engulfed the windows. The metal walls and desert sky dissolved into radiant phosphor.

"Wow..." Pierre breathed softly into the void. <!-- L0968 -->

Cool mountain air washed over his skin. Pierre blinked against the golden daylight, finding himself sprawled upon polished marble flagstones in an open-air colonnade of Doric pillars. Towering cedar shelves lined the perimeter, groaning under leather-bound codices. <!-- L0972 -->

Standing in the center of the rotunda, bathed in daylight, were the Fates—three ancient women hovering over a suspended loom. <!-- L0973 -->

"Oh, s***," Pierre whispered under his breath. <!-- L0974 -->

Draped in heavy linen, their withered hands worked continuously over a suspended wooden loom, silvery threads blurring through gnarled fingers. "Ah, you're early," the first murmured like grinding stones. "Oh no," the second chimed in, "you're late. Come closer... Come closer. This is interesting." <!-- L0975 -->

Pierre scrambled backward until his spine struck a fluted pedestal, clutching his rucksack like a buckler. He was just a totally normal boy with glasses and a nice beret. "So, is... um, is this normal for buses in America to do this type of thing?" Pierre asked hesitantly. <!-- L0977-L0980 -->

Glancing across the flagstones, Pierre spotted the ripped guy in the black tank top pushing himself up from the floor. Definitely not fond of the ripped guy, Pierre pointedly turned to his left toward the professor. <!-- L0982 -->
