## CHAPTER 32: THE CRACKLING VEIL & THE INK-BLOT PROBE

The piercing shriek of the university fire alarm tore through the lecture hall, vibrating through the wooden floorboards and setting the overhead fixtures rattling. Cold air rushed through the high auditorium as panicked students scrambled over desks, knocking down folding chairs in their frantic race toward the lobby. A blinding burst of ozone had erupted the moment Edward Dravin turned the brass key in Dr. Thorne's briefcase, snapping the party out of the 1948 laboratory vision and dropping them straight into chaos. In the second row of the steeply raked seating, the quiet woman in the tailored coat rose to her feet. The mundane human glamour melted from her skin like wax over an open flame. Thick dark hair swirled around her head in a suspended tempest, crackling with arcs of blue-white electrical charge that hummed against the floor. At the three main entrances to the auditorium, heavy double doors banged against the wall stops as three towering satyrs strode inward, cloven hooves thudding on the tiles. <!-- L0217-L0218 -->

Pierre adjusted his round wire spectacles and ducked behind the wooden podium as papers fluttered through the draft. He gripped the smooth shaft of his bronze museum javelin, taking in the tactical layout: exits blocked, horned infiltrators flanking the aisles, and the woman's predatory eyes fixed on the open briefcase containing the altered 1948 trial binder. Pierre raised an open palm from behind the lectern, offering an urgent diplomatic smile. "Excuse me, madame. We could have had a pleasant conversation, you know. We saw your satyr companions with the Fates—we spoke with them! We could be friends." <!-- L0240-L0258:pierre -->

From the second row, the crackling woman tilted her head, her levitating coils of hair hissing with raw planar voltage as she fixed her gaze on Pierre's black woolen beret. "I certainly know you," she said, her voice resonant with unnatural double harmonics that echoed over the blaring siren. "I think I will just take the information from your brain. How does that sound?" <!-- L0259:woman -->

"No," Pierre countered smoothly in his crisp Parisian drawl, his jaw tightening as he stepped out from behind the mahogany lectern. "I do not like that at all, actually. Three satyrs at the doors, and now another rift behind us." He called over his shoulder toward the stage apron: "Professor, do you have a plan for this?" <!-- L0260-L0264:pierre -->

Reality buckled along the rear plaster. With a wet, sucking plop, a fist-sized globular mass of viscous black ink dropped through the fissure and undulated across the floorboards. Pierre vaulted the low partition railing to keep clear of the dazing psychic aura radiating from the second row. Right in front of his boots, the glistening dark mass quivered. Pierre leaned forward and prodded the substance with the blunt ferrule of his spear. The living ink immediately surged upward, clinging to the bronze head like hot asphalt. Pierre pivoted on his heel like a lacrosse player. "If you wish to ride my spear, very well. Let us see how you like the aisle!" With a sharp snap of his wrists, he whipped the javelin forward. <!-- L0271-L0274:pierre -->

The blot tore free with a wet slap, flying wide across the raked rows and striking the shoulder of a fleeing attendant before rolling across an empty wooden desk. Pierre wrinkled his nose in disgust, shaking the haft of his weapon. "I do not know what this is, but you have clearly overcooked it! You should have decreased the heat on the oven. This is like a wheel of burnt molasses!" <!-- L0286-L0296:pierre -->

Along the aisles, the fleeing crowd slowed to a dazed crawl beneath the woman's oppressive psychic suppression field. In the lobby beyond the rear doors, demonstrators who had worn folded foil fedoras tossed their discarded disguises onto the tiles as muscular, horned beast-men blocked the street exits. <!-- L0297-L0314 -->

Up on the stage, Alfie sat upright atop the buckram medical binder inside Dr. Thorne's open briefcase. Her painted seashell button eyes narrowed at the electrical storm. Beside her, Dr. Thorne sat frozen against the desk in shock, while Professor Dravin stood over the podium with his ancient hoplite shield. Alfie gave her oversized museum ballcap a resolute tug, her carved fingers resting near her sewing-needle rapier before tapping the pouches of her belt. <!-- L0315-L0318 -->

"Right then," the driftwood figurine rasped, releasing the bronze buckle. "I thought I'd go with the chaos belt." With a sharp twist of the latch, she prepared to unleash three random planar relics into the hall. <!-- L0318-L0340:alfie -->
