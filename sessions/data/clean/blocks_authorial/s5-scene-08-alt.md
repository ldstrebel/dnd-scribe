"The arts area is all the way across campus," the clipboard-wielding graduate attendant stammered in the arched corridor outside the auditorium doors. <!-- L0940:attendant -->

Pierre stood planted before him, his black beret tilted back. "Do you possess a map?" Pierre demanded, adjusting his round brass spectacles with a sharp snap of his fingers. "A true floorplan of this institute? The aesthetic wing cannot simply dissolve into the ether." <!-- L0941:pierre -->

"Are you even registered for this lecture, sir?" the young attendant pleaded in dazed exasperation. Pierre merely scoffed at such catastrophic faculties before sweeping back into the hall. <!-- L0942-L0944:attendant -->

High in the last amphitheater tier, Eusacles sprawled across his seat behind dark aviators, iron morningstar hidden beneath his coat. "Am I the only one who actually sat down for this damn lecture?" he grunted into his collar, scowling away eager undergrads. <!-- L0956-L0974:eusacles -->

Near the backstage wing, Pierre attempted to creep after Dravin and Alfie, his bronze javelin rattling carelessly against the wainscoting. "Professor!" Pierre hissed in a stage whisper that carried across three rows. "Is all of the faculty in America like this, where they have no clue where the best and most important classes are?" <!-- L0990:pierre -->

Dravin froze in horror at the botched stealth. Pivoting smoothly on his heel, Dravin adopted his fiercest, most intimidating academic dean persona, glaring at Pierre with icy authority and sternly demanding that the young man please take his seat immediately as the presentation was beginning. Pierre threw his hands up in defeat, retreating to an empty aisle chair directly beside the displaced faculty host.

"I shall sit beside you," Pierre whispered conspiratorially, tapping his pencil against his knee. "And when this dismal assembly concludes, we shall embark together to find the sculpture studio." <!-- L0996:pierre -->

"Yes, marvelous—just please be quiet and listen!" the host hissed, clutching his program notes. <!-- L0997:attendant -->

Behind the proscenium, Dravin murmured an arcane syllable of Thaumaturgy, echoing a deep acoustic theater chime through the rafters to command total silence as Dr. Aris Thorne stepped forward. Dravin and Alfie slipped silently through the service door into the private green room. <!-- L1011 -->

"No university caps," Alfie whispered, rooting through the welcome basket on the glass coffee table. "I'll take the soccer scarf. And the fruit." She wound the knit wool three times around her neck, slipping an apple and two oranges into her coat with nautical dexterity. A folded piece of paper sat on the tray beneath the oranges, covered in scribbled notes. <!-- L1022-L1028:alfie -->

Meanwhile, Dravin opened Dr. Thorne's leather travel bag on an armchair. He sifted through folded cardigans, blouses, and a spare stethoscope. "The binder," Dravin murmured, clicking the brass latches shut. "It isn't here. Nothing but personal travel attire." <!-- L1041:dravin -->

Outside on the stage, the muffled cadence of Dr. Thorne's lecture echoed through the wood paneling, followed by audible gasps from the audience. "Consider what Naomi told us," Dravin said under his breath. "A dry academic medical lecture, and every seat in the house is filled. It shouldn't be this popular. That is precisely why she clocked it as a fragment—a place where reality isn't weaving correctly." <!-- L1047-L1051:dravin -->

Alfie stopped, the scarf fringe dangling from her wooden fingers. "Then a fragment isn't necessarily a thing you can hold," she said slowly. "It could just be a moment in time. A room like this one, at the wrong hour." <!-- L1052-L1054:alfie -->

"No," Dravin answered firmly. "Naomi was clear back at the cabin. Reality doesn't unravel into the open air; every tear in the weave anchors to a physical relic, exactly like the Greek stele in Raleigh. We are looking for an object." <!-- L1055-L1064:dravin -->

Peering through the velvet curtain, they looked out onto the illuminated stage. There stood Dr. Aris Thorne before the graph slides. And resting beside her boots at the lectern sat a heavy leather briefcase—sealed with a brass tumbler padlock.
