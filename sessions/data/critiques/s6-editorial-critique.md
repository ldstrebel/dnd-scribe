## 🎭 LENS 1: NARRATIVE CRAFT FEEDBACK (For Creative Engine & Polish Suite)

### 1. Tonal Whiplash: Premature Bathos Undercutting Cosmic Horror
* **The Defect:** Session 6 introduces a terrifying cosmological premise—a Reductor agent deploying psychic tendrils to physically extract and obliterate memories. This is body horror with profound existential stakes.
* **The Narrative Flaw:** The draft undercuts this dread almost instantly with slapstick. When Pierre has his memory of Janette stolen, it is played for broad, theatrical absurdity (*"Our afternoon at the chateau! Where has it gone?! It has become a message board!"*). The loss of Eusacles’s mechanical knowledge is treated as mere HP chip-damage followed by a casual *"Thanks, buddy."*
* **Engine Feedback:** The scene robs the antagonist of menace before she is even defeated. If memory erasure is tragic and horrifying, the comedy must sit *inside* the desperation, not replace it. Pierre's grief needs a beat of genuine disorientation and panic before his theatrical vanity reasserts itself. Eusacles must show visceral vertigo or cognitive emptiness.

### 2. Combat Staging: Saturday Morning Slapstick vs. High-Stakes Threat
* **The Defect:** Scene 7 (Alfie’s Mage Band hair tie) and Scene 8 (Dravin’s glowing three-foot wooden disciplinary ruler).
* **The Narrative Flaw:** While Alfie’s improvised hair-tie logic is clever and true to table intent, Dravin summoning a giant elementary school ruler to paddle satyrs shifts the aesthetic from gothic mystery into a Saturday morning cartoon. It directly conflicts with Dravin's established characterization across Sessions 1–5 (a grave, melancholic academic coping with death and cosmic thresholds).
* **Engine Feedback:** Ground the spectral weapon in Dravin's stern academic authority without trivializing combat peril. A heavy hardwood straightedge striking with the concussive force of an executioner's bar feels dangerous; describing it as a classroom paddle for knuckle-slapping turns lethal satyrs into naughty schoolchildren.

### 3. Environmental Hazards as Mechanical Checklists
* **The Defect:** Scenes 4–5 (Sprinklers, electrified flooring, broken glass).
* **The Narrative Flaw:** The draft lists environmental elements sequentially like a D&D grid map: the water falls, the electricity arcs, the desk provides cover. The prose lacks visceral sensory claustrophobia—the blinding spray in the eyes, the smell of burning wet wool, the deafening alarm bell drowning out voices, the terror of slipping on tile while holding a metal spear.
* **Engine Feedback:** Stop staging hazards as separate turn events. Integrate the environment into the physical exertion of every motion. Characters should be struggling against the terrain, not just observing it between strikes.

### 4. The Antiflimactic Resolution Drag
* **The Defect:** Scene 10 (Janitor cart stashing, Dr. Thorne manipulation, model inspection).
* **The Narrative Flaw:** After defeating the woman, the scene completely loses narrative velocity. Stashing an unconscious enemy in a janitor cart and leisurely debating architectural models stalls the forward momentum.
* **Engine Feedback:** The post-combat resolution needs urgency. Campus security is coming, the alarm is howling, and Dr. Thorne is a volatile liability. Tighten the dialogue by 30%, emphasize the ticking clock of discovery, and cut straight into the retreat to the Lost Roads.

### 5. Actionable Creative Engine Punch-List
1. **Scene 6-7 Memory Loss Beat:** Add 2 sentences of visceral disorientation (cold vacuum behind the eyes, auditory drop) before Pierre's vanity wail.
2. **Scene 8 Ruler Staging:** Re-frame Dravin's spectral straightedge from "classroom schoolmaster paddle" to a rigid heavy surveyor bar of divine threshold law.
3. **Scene 10 Transit Trim:** Compress the custodial cart debate by 40 words to sharpen escape velocity into the Lost Roads portal.

## 🎲 LENS 2: PLAYER & GAME MASTER TABLETOP FEEDBACK (For Table Coaching)

### 1. The Game Master (Luke Foreman)
* **Encounter Over-Engineering vs. Narrative Clarity:**
  - *The Flaw:* Combining psychic extraction, a fire alarm, ceiling deluge, electrified puddle hazards, and two Satyr adds in a single lecture room overwhelmed player cognitive bandwidth. 
  - *Table Impact:* The players spent ~25% of their table talk asking logistical grid questions (*"If I step into the water do I get shocked?"*, *"Is she behind the desk or on the desk?"*) rather than acting in character.
  - *Coaching:* When introducing complex environmental hazards, establish clear visual zones immediately. Telegraph psychic attacks with sensory cues (smell of burnt hair, temporal echo) rather than stating mechanics like *"she's taking a memory."*
* **Post-Combat Pacing Collapse:**
  - *The Flaw:* Once the woman was down, the GM allowed the table to meander in circular deliberations about hostage transport and custodial carts.
  - *Coaching:* Inject immediate external pressure to force a swift decision. Don't let players debate logistics in a room with ringing fire alarms and sprinkler showers; bring footsteps down the hall.

### 2. Pierre’s Player (Luke S)
* **The Comedic Reflex Trap:**
  - *The Flaw:* Pierre's default instinct when faced with peril is instantaneous comic deflection (acid hair rinses, French chateau laments, *"Pierre is a lover, not a fighter"*).
  - *Character Consistency:* While Pierre’s vanity and cowardice are his defining traits, leaning on them too early erases the danger for the entire party. If Pierre is never genuinely frightened, the monsters are never scary.
  - *Coaching:* Allow Pierre to experience authentic terror for one beat before retreating into vanity. Panic makes vanity funny; vanity without panic makes combat inconsequential. Also, stop attempting to negotiate parleys after initiative is rolled and weapons are drawn.

### 3. Professor Dravin’s Player (William Webb)
* **Tactical Over-Deliberation & Archetype Drift:**
  - *The Flaw:* Spends excessive time calculating optimal spell actions and grid positioning, causing combat turns to drag.
  - *Character Consistency:* Manifesting a disciplinary school ruler for *Spiritual Weapon* was a reach for table laughter that broke Dravin's established solemnity. Dravin is a grieving academic fascinated by death and threshold theology, not an angry schoolmaster paddling truant students.
  - *Coaching:* Commit to spells decisively. Ground Dravin's magical manifestations in his deep, melancholic scholarly obsessions (the weight of tombstones, theological texts, funerary bells), not prop gags.

### 4. Alfie’s Player (Sophie Foreman Noone)
* **Physical Ecology & Puppet Peril:**
  - *The Flaw:* Great improvisational instincts (the *Mage Band* hair tie was brilliant), but consistently forgets Alfie's physical fragility. Alfie is an 18-inch carved wooden driftwood doll in a pool of water with active electrical currents.
  - *Table Impact:* Alfie acts with the tactical nonchalance of a 200-lb barbarian, diving under desks and trading quips.
  - *Coaching:* Play into the terrifying reality of being made of wood and dry straw. Waterlogging slows him down; electricity chars his limbs; satyr hooves can splinter him into kindling. A fragile character showing audacity is heroic; an invulnerable doll making quips is just noise.

### 5. Eusacles’s Player (John Hagey)
* **Chronic Non-Combat Passivity:**
  - *The Flaw:* Eusacles is reliable in combat (positioning, morningstar strikes), but almost entirely vanishes during social encounters, tactical planning, and the aftermath.
  - *Roleplay Miss:* When Eusacles had his motorcycle memory ripped out, the player said almost nothing and showed zero roleplay reaction beyond taking the damage.
  - *Coaching:* Demand spotlight outside of combat. When your character suffers cognitive violation or memory theft, RP the disorientation! Ask questions, express confusion, show the psychological wound. Don't let Pierre and Dravin dominate 85% of the conversational airtime.
