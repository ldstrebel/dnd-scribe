## CHAPTER 11: THE BONFIRE AT BLOCK 99

Night fell over Vumbua with the sudden, damp chill of the northern sea. Across the sprawling basin, the repurposed expedition campus transformed into a labyrinth of shadows and glowing sparks. Seventy thousand candidates, adrift in the bewildering sprawl of the academy on their first night, had begun congregating in pockets of warmth. Wherever the turf had not yet taken root or where timber scraps lay stacked beside unfinished scaffolds, fires were being raised against the dark. <!-- L0306 --> <!-- L0308 -->

Above them, the sky was alive with movement. Airships with glowing signal lanterns glided on silent thermal currents between the spires, their hulls gleaming brass-yellow against the stars, while down in the basin, cutters and steam launches carved white wakes across the black water. From the western bluffs, the sound of orchestral music and bright glass lanterns drifted down from the grand manors where aristocratic scions held court. But down in the dust of the lower quads, the common candidates made do with construction scrap and camaraderie. <!-- L0310 -->

Ignatius stood in the shadows near Block 99, black traveling coat dusted with chalky soot, watching the small clusters of students pass by. <!-- L0312 --> <!-- L0313 -->

A few paces away, Lomi approached the circle, flat cap pushed back on his brow, wiping machine grease from his knuckles with a rag. He had spent the last twenty minutes observing a growing crisis at the central pit. <!-- L0314 --> <!-- L0315 --> <!-- L0316 -->

"Hey, Ignatius," Lomi called out with an easy grin, nodding back over his shoulder toward the industrial yard. "Good to see you again. They could use a little help over by the pit. I don't mean to insinuate anything, but I think you could get a fire going if you needed to." <!-- L0317 --> <!-- L0320 --> <!-- L0322 --> <!-- L0327 --> <!-- L0335 --> <!-- L0337 -->

Ignatius crossed his arms, raising a solitary eyebrow while tiny crimson embers danced lazily along the fringe of his hair. "Let me get this straight. You think that just because I come from the Ember Isles that you can just stroll over here and ask me for a light whenever you need one? You're not wrong, but that's not the point." <!-- L0338 -->

Lomi chuckled, pulling a dry twist of waste paper from his canvas pocket. "I wasn't saying you had to perform. Just figured I'd bring a scrap of paper and a polite request." <!-- L0341 --> <!-- L0343 -->

"Well, honestly," Ignatius said, the corner of his mouth ticking upward, "I'm glad you trust me so much right out of the gate." <!-- L0344 -->

"There's just something about you that screams 'good at starting fires,'" Lomi said. "Can't imagine why." <!-- L0345 -->

Ignatius took the paper twist, letting the warmth of his thumb char the edge into a steady, glowing coal. "Yeah, yeah. Sure. Let's go see what they're struggling with." <!-- L0346 -->

They strolled over to the fire pit, where a crowd of shivering candidates had gathered around a nervous young student in thick brass spectacles. The boy was kneeling in the dirt, clutching a clipboard covered in manic geometric diagrams, adjusting every piece of lumber with millimeter precision. He had laid out a hyper-symmetrical pyramid of dry pine, but refused to let anyone near it until his draft calculations balanced. <!-- L0349 --> <!-- L0351 -->

Ignatius didn't wait for the math. With a casual flick of his wrist, he tossed the burning scrap directly into the heart of the kindling. <!-- L0352 -->

A hungry *whoosh* tore through the yard. The pine caught instantly, leaping five feet into the air in a roaring geyser of orange sparks.

The bespectacled boy shrieked, tumbling backward onto his elbows. "No! No, no, no! The draft was calibrated for ignition from the western quadrant! What is this? This is entirely non-optimal!" He scrambled to his feet, squinting through the heat at Lomi. "Explain yourself, please! Why have you done this?"

Lomi rested his hands on his hips, totally unfazed by the academic outrage. "In my experience down in the boiler rooms, every minute a furnace isn't burning is money out of your pocket. Time is money, kid. You focus on uptime, whether it's pretty or not. Perfect is the enemy of good. We needed a fire, now we got a fire." <!-- L0354 --> <!-- L0356 -->

The boy stared at the leaping flames, his mouth opening and closing like a landed perch. He looked down at his ruined clipboard, then back at the roaring blaze, grudgingly adjusting his spectacles. "An interesting proposition. I will... consider this. It was effective, certainly, though it is not always true that momentum is life, regardless of what the radio announcers preach." He cleared his throat, straightening his crumpled tunic. "I am Cassius. Given that you have successfully concluded an operational objective, you are entitled to know." <!-- L0359 --> <!-- L0373 -->

"Lomi," the mechanic replied, taking the boy's hand with a firm, calloused grip. "Worked down in the Deep-Hull's boilers for years. It's nice to see what you boys are designing up here top-side." <!-- L0374 --> <!-- L0376 --> <!-- L0378 -->

"I am merely a first-year student," Cassius mumbled, cheeks flushing a violent crimson as his gaze darted past Lomi toward the edge of the courtyard. "I... I just felt this was the place to be tonight." <!-- L0379 --> <!-- L0402 -->

Lomi caught the boy's furtive glance instantly. Standing thirty paces back near the crane footings was Serra Vox, clutching her field notes and observing the fire with bright, predatory curiosity. Lomi clapped Cassius hard across the shoulder, speaking in a hearty, booming voice designed to carry. "Well, man, I don't see how we would've gotten warmth tonight without you! You laid a great foundation, Cassius! Beautiful work!" <!-- L0403 --> <!-- L0406 -->

Cassius stumbled forward from the force of the pat, beaming with flustered pride. <!-- L0411 -->

At the edge of the light, Britt and Aggie watched the blaze with quiet wariness. To beings of bark, soil, and living mycelium, a roaring fire was never a simple comfort; it was an ancient predator that consumed the forest floor. Aggie hovered just behind Britt's shoulder, her dark eyes tracking the leaping sparks with cautious fascination. <!-- L0417 -->

Serra Vox, meanwhile, had crept up beside them, practically vibrating with anthropological excitement. She chewed her lip, opened her mouth to ask a question, hesitated, and fidgeted with her pen. <!-- L0421 --> <!-- L0423 -->

"Are you all right?" Britt asked, turning a cool, level gaze upon her. <!-- L0426 -->

"Oh gosh, is it that obvious?" Serra blurted out, her composure crumbling into breathless enthusiasm. "I'm sorry, I really didn't mean to stare! It's just... this is the most remarkable gathering of disparate cultures since the Treaty of the Spires! I'm completely overwhelmed. Where are you from? I mean—have you seen maps of the continent? Oh, that was rude, I didn't mean to assume—" <!-- L0427 -->

"We've seen some maps," Britt answered evenly. "Our clan lives in the southern canopies. We don't get much outside news." <!-- L0430 -->

"Well, the world is enormous!" Serra explained, gesturing broadly toward the north. "The Seat of Harmony alone holds eight million citizens! It's colossal—far too noisy for my taste, honestly, but it's where all the great expeditions were launched. That's why being here at Vumbua feels so historic. We're on the edge of a new age!" <!-- L0431 --> <!-- L0433 --> <!-- L0435 -->

## CHAPTER 12: COURTYARD CLUSTERS & HIGH-HOUSE ROW

Around the warmth of the roaring blaze, the circle settled into uneasy familiarity. Serra Vox flipped open her compact notebook, pen poised like a surgeon's scalpel as she studied Britt and Aggie. <!-- L0465 -->

"Why did you say living in the capital was unfortunate?" Britt asked, leaning her heavy shoulders against a weathered pine timber. "Isn't the Seat of Harmony where everyone wants to go?" <!-- L0467 --> <!-- L0469 -->

Serra glanced down at her boots, a shadow passing over her usually manic features. "There is... immense political pressure there," she said quietly, her voice losing its academic bounce. "The weight of expectations, the lineages, the old families constantly maneuvering for battery contracts and prestige. It is exhausting. I far prefer the frontier settlements where people care about practical matters rather than genealogies." <!-- L0470 --> <!-- L0472 -->

"Why were you there then?" Britt pressed.

Serra gave a tight, evasive little smile, snapping her notebook shut with a sharp *clack*. "Everyone finds a reason to pass through the Seat eventually. But look—the fire is marvelous, isn't it?" She sprang to her feet, visibly eager to escape the personal interrogation, and hurried toward the hearth where Cassius was still hovering near the logs. "Cassius! Did you arrange this yourself?" <!-- L0482 -->

"He did," Ignatius called out from across the circle, flashing a wide, mischievous grin. "Did it pretty much all on his own. Natural pyrotechnic genius." <!-- L0483 -->

"Couldn't have managed without him," Lomi echoed, winking. "We just provided moral support." <!-- L0486 --> <!-- L0488 -->

Cassius blushed furiously, bowing with stiff formality before retreating toward the far side of the timber to avoid Serra's gaze. Ignatius ambled over to where the young scholar sat trembling, leaning down with an exaggeratedly confidential whisper. <!-- L0520 --> <!-- L0523 -->

"Hey," Ignatius murmured, elbowing the boy lightly. "I overheard her talking to the girls. She said she's obsessed with anthropology. That means she's really into ants. You should go tell her about ants." <!-- L0524 -->

Cassius looked up, horrified. "That... that is fundamentally not what anthropology means." <!-- L0525 -->

"Hey, don't overthink it, kid," Ignatius chuckled. "Just go strike up a conversation. Work with what you've got." <!-- L0526 --> <!-- L0530 -->

Lomi leaned in from the other side, patting the scholar's shoulder. "He's right, Cassius. There's definitely a spark between you two." <!-- L0537 --> <!-- L0539 -->

Cassius gasped, slapping at his woolen coat in panic. "A spark?! Did you push me too close to the coals? Am I burning?" <!-- L0540 --> <!-- L0542 -->

"You're fine, kid," Lomi laughed, catching him by the collar before he tumbled into the ash. "Just an expression. Relax." <!-- L0544 -->

With Cassius sufficiently flustered and Serra furiously sketching log angles, the newcomers took stock of the wider quad. Beyond the warm perimeter of Block 99's pit, the campus was divided into distinct social currents. To the south, where the industrial docks met the canal, a loud gathering of roughnecks and grease-monkeys had surrounded a crateside vendor. Above the din, a familiar, fast-talking voice cut through the damp air like a rusted saw blade—Lucky, standing atop an overturned munitions case, pulling gleaming trinkets from a greasy leather satchel. <!-- L0545 --> <!-- L0547 --> <!-- L0551 -->

"Look at that commotion over by the bluffs," Ignatius said suddenly, his attention shifting past the peddler toward the dark cliffs on the western rim. <!-- L0554 --> <!-- L0556 -->

Just beyond the reach of the firelight, a sharp altercation had broken out beneath a cluster of iron lampposts. A tall, impeccably tailored gentleman was waving a silver-headed cane in the face of a hulking, clockwork automaton whose brass chassis was scratched and soot-stained. Beside them, a wiry figure resembling woven vines was gesturing wildly, while a young woman with stark platinum-blonde hair stood between them, her voice cutting through the mist like cold glass.

"Hey, Lomi," Ignatius muttered, his embers brightening along his shoulders. "You hearing that?" <!-- L0562 --> <!-- L0564 -->

"Hard not to," Lomi replied, eyes narrowing as he adjusted his flat cap. "Looks like a proper scrap brewing." <!-- L0565 -->

Behind them, Britt and Aggie exchanged a glance. Aggie tilted her red cap toward the lampposts, curious and alert. <!-- L0566 -->

"Let's see what's got them stirred up," Britt said, stepping away from the fire. "Where there's shouting, there's usually something people want to hide." <!-- L0567 --> <!-- L0569 --> <!-- L0570 -->

## CHAPTER 13: LUCKY'S LUCIDIAN TRADE & THE MISSING CLANS

The argument beneath the lampposts was drawing a small crowd. Standing at the center of the dispute was a young noble dressed in a ruffled silk cravat and embroidered waistcoat, waving an ornate silver cane to punctuate his points. <!-- L0574 -->

"Listen to me!" the young gentleman insisted, leaning forward on the balls of his boots. "I'll wager fifty gold sovereigns right now that there is an subterranean Earth clan! The ancient charts require it! We have two confirmed clans—Ash-Blood and Mizizi. There have to be at least four more waiting beyond the frontier!"

"Nonsense," grunted a student in dark working leather. "We found fire and plants. That's probably all that survived the Collapse. You're chasing fairy tales." <!-- L0576 -->

"Look at the symmetry!" the noble shouted back. "Fire, flora, water, earth, air! It is an elementary resonance circuit! If the old world operated on harmony, the nodes must balance!"

A quiet student whose skin was layered in flexible green bark spoke softly from the shadows. "What if they are not the missing? What if we are the ones who forgot?"

A young woman with stark, wind-sheared platinum hair crossed her arms, rolling her eyes. "Or maybe the world is just broken. Not everything needs a neat little pattern. And besides, who would want to live buried in the dark under miles of dirt? No sky, no storms to chase—sounds like misery." <!-- L0629 -->

"Mark my words," the noble declared, tapping his cane against the cobblestones. "When the deep expeditions locate the subterranean clan, I will be vindicated!"

Lomi pushed his way to the front of the circle, thumbs hooked casually into his canvas suspenders. "I don't mean to butt in on high academic theory," he said, drawing amused glances from the students. "But down in the Deep-Hull's furnace decks, we hear things. You spend twelve hours shovel-feeding a boiler beside the hull plates, and you start feeling rhythms vibrating up through the bedrock. Rhythms that don't belong to steam engines or ocean tides. There are old tales among the union crews about subterranean folk who watch the deep shafts."

The noble beamed, extending a silk-gloved hand. "See! The working classes know! Percival Van Smite the Third. A pleasure, sir."

"Lomi Sultano," the mechanic replied, shaking the offered hand. "Boilermaker union. Third generation."

## CHAPTER 14: THE CYCLE OF LIFE & DEATH

The circle around the fire grew contemplative as the warmth seeped into cold joints. Serra sat with her knees pulled to her chest, chin resting on her forearms, watching the embers pulse. <!-- L0702 -->

"Do you talk about death in your nursery tales?" Serra asked, her tone hushed and curious. "In Harmony, we hide death behind rhymes and polite ceremonies. Why speak of it so openly?"

"Why hide the truth?" Aggie replied softly, her voice small but steady. "Death isn't an enemy. It leads to more life. Everything dies eventually. Maybe that's a sorrow, but everything that dies comes back in the cycle. Maybe I'll return as a great iron-bark tree, or a meadow flower. I like being a mushroom for now, though." <!-- L0706 --> <!-- L0709 --> <!-- L0710 -->

Serra sighed, staring into the flames. "My father is aging, and he would rather discuss anything in the world than his own mortality. Hearing you speak of it with such quiet peace is... refreshing." She paused, turning her bright, inquisitive gaze toward Ignatius. "Do either of you understand what it takes for a new node to integrate with Harmony?" <!-- L0712 --> <!-- L0714 -->

"Not particularly," Britt admitted.

Ignatius grabbed a rounded, fist-sized chunk of dark volcanic basalt from his heavy canvas pocket and swung it casually from palm to palm; tiny wisps of steam curled from his calloused skin, but the fabric of his coat remained untouched. "I keep this with me," he said. <!-- L0715 --> <!-- L0717 -->

Serra's eyes widened. "Doesn't that burn through your pockets?" <!-- L0719 -->

Ignatius laughed, shaking his head. "You think our clan spent three thousand years living in the throat of an active caldera and never figured out how to weave heat-resistant thread? We don't sweat like you mainlanders to cool down. If an Ash-Blood gets too warm, we vent the heat thermodynamically—we burn hotter, and transfer the excess energy straight into the atmosphere." <!-- L0720 --> <!-- L0724 --> <!-- L0730 --> <!-- L0732 -->

Cassius leaped from the bench, scrambled for his dropped pen, and lunged forward to scribble notes against his knee as Ignatius spoke. <!-- L0735 -->

"The rocks only provide raw thermal energy," Ignatius continued, nodding toward the glowing stone. "We boil water, cook food, and forge metal with them. It's not stable and humming like the crystal batteries your magistrates carry. But here's the mystery: when Harmony's explorers first landed on our shores two years ago, our dying volcanoes suddenly flared back to life. We thought our fires were cooling forever. Then Harmony arrived, and the heat surged." <!-- L0738 --> <!-- L0740 -->

Britt leaned forward, her shell plates creaking softly. "What did your clan gain from the alliance?" <!-- L0742 -->

"Trade," Ignatius said. "We give them hot stone; they give us crystal batteries. And our clan matriarch, Lady Ignis, was granted an official seat in the Council of Harmony. The empire claims that the newest civilization should be given the greatest voice, so the least of us becomes the most of us." He let out a dry, cynical puff of smoke. "Of course, because our elders don't know the first thing about managing an eight-million-person metropolis, the magistrates pull the strings anyway." <!-- L0749 --> <!-- L0753 --> <!-- L0755 --> <!-- L0762 --> <!-- L0781 -->

Aggie sat in silence, turning Ignatius's words over in her mind. *Two years ago.* The exact moment Harmony landed on the Ash-Blood shores and ignited their dying fires was the exact moment the southern forest stopped decaying. The empire had flooded the Mizizi with iron axes and saws, desperate to force the petrified trees into rot so they could dissect the mechanism of decay. <!-- L0802 --> <!-- L0804 -->

A cheerful clatter broke the reverie as Percival returned, his silk waistcoat slightly askew as he strutted back into the circle.

"And that," Percival announced grandly to the fire, "is precisely why there must be more clans! The ancient grid demands completion!" He halted, his gaze landing on Ignatius's smoldering hair, then darting to Britt and Aggie. "Great heavens! I had no idea our gathering was so distinguished!"

He stepped forward, vigorously pumping Ignatius's hand before turning to inspect Aggie with unabashed aristocratic curiosity. "A pleasure, sir! And madam—I must ask: what is your garment woven from? It resembles living fungus!" <!-- L0808 -->

"It's my shell," Aggie replied matter-of-factly. "Except for my belt, I don't wear clothes."

"In high society, infants wear nothing and the elderly wear whatever they please, but those of us in between are condemned to spend thousands of sovereigns on suffocating wool." <!-- L0818 --> <!-- L0824 -->
