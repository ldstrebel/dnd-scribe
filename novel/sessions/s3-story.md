## CHAPTER 28: THE WALK TO THE CELESTIAL LOUNGE & CARTOGRAPHY

The night deepened past two in the morning, turning the damp streets of Vumbua quiet and still. Most of the seventy thousand candidates had long since collapsed into their temporary barracks or huddled beside dying embers in the lower quads. But for a scattered few, the restless thrum of an unfamiliar world kept sleep at bay. <!-- L0013 --> <!-- L0014 --> <!-- L0053 -->

Loami led the squad along the curving flagstone terraces toward the Upper Core, his flat cap tilted back, hands shoved deep into his pockets. "I've spent ten years working the swing shift in the Deep-Hull's boilers," he said with an easy shrug. "Two in the morning is just when the steam pressure settles. I'm a night owl." <!-- L0054 -->

Beside him, Ignatius walked with buoyant, tireless strides, tiny sparks flickering at the tips of his fingers. "Everything is too new to sleep," he said, dark eyes gleaming. "You don't cross the ocean just to lie in a cot."

Britt, however, trailed half a step behind, her bark-grained shoulders stiff, dark amber eyes heavy with fatigue. Back in the ancient groves of the Mazizi, life followed the sun; rising with dawn and resting when twilight veiled the canopy was sacred order. Staying awake in an artificial city lit by humming resonance lamps felt perverse. Aggie hovered beside her, sensing her sister's prickly reluctance, while Iggy trotted quietly in the rear, his woolen trench coat buttoned to his chin and round goggles reflecting the gaslights.

At the crest of the limestone bluff stood the Celestial Lounge. Outside its arched mahogany doors, a tall man in an immaculate black butler's livery stood guard, brass buttons gleaming in the torchlight. He eyed their dirt-dusted boots and grease-stained cuffs with aristocratic detachment, but as Loami stepped forward with easy confidence, the attendant merely bowed and pushed the double doors wide. <!-- L0055 --> <!-- L0056 -->

The upper floor opened into an expansive, glass-domed observatory. Gaslights burning behind tinted vanilla crystal cast warm, amber shadows across velvet chaises and low mahogany tables. Across the room, exhausted scions and upperclassmen lay sprawled in slumber, half-empty crystal flutes scattered on sideboards. But it was not the luxury that took the squad's breath away; it was the panoramic glass wall curving along the eastern perimeter. <!-- L0057 --> <!-- L0058 --> <!-- L0059 --> <!-- L0060 -->

Loami and Britt were drawn to the glass as if pulled by gravity. Below them, the sprawling harbor complex stretched across the basin in an intricate grid of steam and lantern light. The massive silhouette of the Deep-Hull was anchored in the harbor basin, steam stacks venting white plumes against the black sea. To the west, the glowing envelope of the Sky Spire hung suspended in the clouds, tethered by steel cables that gleamed like spider silk. Half a million lives, countless lanterns, and endless industrial conduits hummed beneath them. <!-- L0061 --> <!-- L0062 --> <!-- L0063 --> <!-- L0064 -->

"Look at that," Loami murmured, pressing a calloused palm against the cold glass. "That's where the steam lines run. That's the power plant down by Block 99. You can see the whole layout from up here... like looking down at a living blueprint." <!-- L0066 -->

Aggie stood beside Britt, her lips parting in silent awe. "Whoa..." she whispered, the sheer scale of surface civilization pressing upon her senses like a physical weight.

Ignatius didn't just look—he immediately unbuttoned his traveler's tunic, pulled out his leatherbound field notebook, and began sketching furiously. Beside him, Iggy hopped onto a cushioned stool, using the elevated vantage to trace the transit roads, harbor locks, and structural conduits Loami pointed out. In twenty minutes of quiet, feverish scribbling, the two of them mapped the topography of the spires with remarkable precision. <!-- L0067 --> <!-- L0068 --> <!-- L0069 -->

"Hey! You!" a loud, cheerful voice called across the lounge. <!-- L0070 -->

Ignatius looked up. Striding toward them was an aristocratic young man in an embroidered silver-and-black tunic, waving with exuberant familiarity. It was Valentine "Val" Sterling—the cadet Ignatius had encountered during the entrance examination. Behind him, seated near a sound-baffled broadcast studio lined with audio crystals, was Ember, Ignatius's sharp-tongued Ash-Blood cousin, observing the room with cool, calculating detachment. <!-- L0071 --> <!-- L0075 --> <!-- L0080 -->

## CHAPTER 29: CROWN AND RUIN & THE STOLEN STUDY GUIDE

"I knew I could find someone awake!" Val proclaimed, pulling Ignatius toward a curved mahogany gambling table in the center of the lounge. "You look like a man of culture, Ignatius. Up for a wager? We could play Truths and Treachery, or we could play Crown and Ruin. My cousin Valerius passed out on the velvet settee after four rounds, but you have the look of someone who can hold his spirits."

Loami sauntered over, flat cap tilted back, eyes gleaming with competitive mischief. "Crown and Ruin? How do the cards fall?"

"No cards—dice," Val grinned, pulling a velvet pouch from his pocket and rolling out pairs of faceted d12s. "A gold Hope die and a purple Fear die. You roll both. If Hope beats Fear, you bank the difference toward ten points. But if Fear reigns higher... you mark a point of stress and down a shot." <!-- L0081 --> <!-- L0083 --> <!-- L0084 -->

Loami chuckled, reaching inside his heavy canvas work jacket and unscrewing the brass cap of a battered steel flask. "If we're taking penalty shots, let's skip the watered-down wine. This is straight turbine distillate from the lower boilers. Sharp enough to strip varnish off oak." <!-- L0096 -->

Ember slid off her stool in the broadcast booth, crossing her arms with a cold smirk. "If Ignatius is playing, I'm at the table."

The dice clattered across the polished green felt. Round after round, laughter and groans echoed through the quiet lounge as the purple dice bit back. Val went down in flames early, rolling three consecutive Fear spikes and coughing violently as he choked down Loami's boiler grease. Ember held her ground until a pair of sixes busted her bank, sending her storming back to her soundboard in disgust. But it was Iggy, leaning over the felt with his copper goggles askew, who rolled a miraculous double-twelve—a perfect Crown—capping his score at ten and claiming victory. <!-- L0102 --> <!-- L0104 -->

"Incredible," Val slurred, resting his forehead against the green baize, his silver embroidery stained with grease. "A prodigy of the dirt."

Loami leaned in, resting his elbows on the table and pouring Val a glass of water. "Speaking of prodigies... what was that you were bragging about earlier? Something about the entrance exams?" <!-- L0105 -->

Val gave a loose, drunken wave of his hand. "Oh, the first-year diagnostic. Scheduled for the end of the week. Everyone's terrified of it. So... I wrote a test. A brutal, punishing mock examination." <!-- L0106 -->

"You wrote it?" Ignatius asked, brow furrowing.

"Sold it to Lucky as a study guide," Val mumbled, his eyelids fluttering. "Didn't want gold for it. Told Lucky he had to trade it for stories or favors... keep it accessible. If students can solve my problems, they'll have the fundamentals down cold. I just wanted... wanted people to be prepared..." His chin struck the table, and within seconds, a gentle snore rose from the felt. <!-- L0107 --> <!-- L0108 --> <!-- L0110 -->

Loami straightened, looking across the table at Ignatius and Iggy. "Lucky has the exam answers. We need to find Lucky." <!-- L0111 -->

At the edge of the room, Britt had watched the game with growing impatience. Near the exit, three men had stood up from a corner booth—two towering, muscular bruisers with intricate dreadlocks and silver piercings, led by a slender, sharply dressed man in an urban wool trench coat. They were communicating in silent, fluid sign language, their expressions grim and burdened with sorrow. As they pushed through the mahogany doors into the night, Britt made her decision. Turning on her heel, she slipped out into the corridor to follow them, desperate for directions back to Block 99. A dozen paces behind, Iggy trotted into the shadows to keep her in sight. <!-- L0112 --> <!-- L0118 --> <!-- L0120 --> <!-- L0121 --> <!-- L0126 --> <!-- L0127 -->

## CHAPTER 30: THE AMBER AMBUSH IN THE ALLEY

Outside the Celestial Lounge, the limestone alleys curved into the deeper, shadowed folds of the upper city. Britt walked a few paces behind the three men, her boots clicking softly against the damp paving. Up close, the two flanking escorts were massive, their broad shoulders draped in rough-spun cloaks, intricate dark dreadlocks swaying against silver piercings in their ears. They moved in uncanny silence, communicating with fluid, practiced hand signs that cut through the dark without a whisper.

The smaller man in front—slender, wearing a tailored wool overcoat—paused beneath a guttering gas lamp and turned with an easy, practiced smile. "You said Block 99, sister? You're a first-year candidate?"

"Yes," Britt said, her voice dry with fatigue. "I'm Britt. Just trying to find my bunk before morning."

"I'm Azer," the man replied smoothly, gesturing for her to follow him down an archway leading into an unlit passage between two monolithic lecture halls. "I know this city inside and out. Attempted the candidacy run myself a few seasons back. Didn't make the cut, but I know the shortcuts."

A hundred paces back, Iggy padded through the fog, trying to keep Britt's silhouette in view. But as Azer led her deeper into the labyrinth, the turns became erratic—a sharp right down a narrow drainage sluice, a sudden left beneath a flying buttress. It wasn't a direct route to campus; it was a game of evasion. Iggy scrambled down a cobblestone ramp, trying to anticipate their trajectory, but on an unfamiliar terrace, his boots slipped on wet moss. By the time he righted himself, the misty alley ahead was empty.

In the blind alley, Azer came to a sudden halt before a dead-end stone wall. He turned, hands resting lightly in his coat pockets. "Well," he said softly, his voice losing its warmth. "I think we lost your little shadow." He bowed his head with mocking remorse. "I really am sorry about this, sister."

Before Britt could draw breath, a violent, crushing impact exploded against the base of her skull. Two heavy lead saps struck from the shadows behind her with bone-splintering force—nineteen points of raw, savage momentum that rattled her teeth and sent fire blooming across her vision. Instinctively, Britt threw her weight forward, the ancient bark-grain of her skin tightening as she channeled every ounce of ancestral resilience, burning all four of her protective armor lattices to absorb the blow. The shock wave tore through her, dropping her to her knees, blood welling in her mouth, but the shell held.

Weighted cordage whipped through the dark. A heavy hemp net dropped over her shoulders, entangling her arms as the two silent bruisers pinned her against the stones. Calloused hands tore into her pockets, rifling through her travel kit.

Fighting through the ringing in her ears, Britt focused inward. Drawing upon the living mycelial pulse that connected her to her grove, she forged a frantic mental tether through the stones, burning with sharp urgency: *Right. Left. Down the sluice. Find Iggy. Ambush.*

With a feral snarl of bark and sinew, Britt planted her boots and heaved upward, snapping the net cords with raw, desperate strength. But as she broke free, the taller bruiser tore her candidate registration receipt from her coat lining, brandishing the stamped copper-bordered paper.

Azer peered into the torn lining, his sneer deepening into disgust. "Nothing? Not a single brass sovereign? Did they send you from your backwater jungle with empty pockets?" Then his sharp gaze dropped to her throat, catching the glint of a dark wooden pendant resting against her collarbone—a petrified acorn shell, carved from the sacred heartwood of her ancestral tree. "Wait. That might fetch a price in the antique stalls."

Azer lunged. His first swipe missed as Britt jerked away, but his second hand closed over the cord, snapping the sinew and ripping the sacred heirloom from her neck.

"Nickel! Tus! The receipt!" Azer hissed. The two giants turned and sprinted north into the fog with the paper, while Azer bolted south down the alley toward the thoroughfare.

Britt didn't hesitate. The receipt was paper; the pendant was her soul. Snarling, her face contorted with ancient fury, she gave chase. Despite the heavy, deliberate gait of her turtle-kin heritage, she surged across the cobbles with terrifying speed, her dark hair flying like a storm. <!-- L0151 -->

Azer burst out of the alley onto the broad flagstones of the avenue, gasping for air—and froze. Standing directly in the center of the road, looking mildly surprised behind round copper goggles, was Iggy. <!-- L0153 -->

Before Azer could skirt around the earth-kin, a shadow descended upon him like a falling oak. Britt hurled herself forward, her shoulder striking Azer squarely in the spine. They slammed into the flagstones in a violent tangle of limbs and flying dust. In a heartbeat, Britt was on top of him, knees pinned hard against his biceps, one hand gripping his throat while the other pressed the razor-honed tip of her chipped flint dagger directly against his jugular. Her chest heaved with animal fury, her eyes burning like black coals. <!-- L0154 --> <!-- L0155 --> <!-- L0156 --> <!-- L0157 -->

Iggy blinked, took two steps backward, and quietly seated himself upon a flat granite mounting block. He folded his hands in his lap, nodded politely to the night air, and decided Britt had the situation entirely under control. <!-- L0158 -->

## CHAPTER 31: AZER'S INTERROGATION & THE PLAN FOR LUCKY

Heavy footsteps thundered down the cobblestone thoroughfare. Aggie rounded the corner at a frantic sprint, clay-dusted tunic askew, followed closely by Loami and Ignatius. When they saw Britt straddling a pinned man with her flint dagger pressed against his throat, Aggie gasped in relief, rushing forward and planting a heavy boot firmly upon the thief's chest. <!-- L0166 --> <!-- L0167 -->

"Britt! Are you hurt?" Aggie demanded, dark eyes wide with sisterly terror.

"He took my pendant," Britt growled through clenched teeth. With a violent jerk of her wrist, she snatched the sacred petrified acorn necklace from Azer's trembling fingers, clasping the dark wood to her chest until her knuckles turned white. Then, leaning down until her breath ruffled his hair, she caught the silver chain looped to his waistcoat, ripping his engraved pocket watch free with a sharp snap of thread. "You took something of mine. I take something of yours." <!-- L0168 -->

Azer winced, lifting two fingers to gently push the flat of Britt's blade away from his skin. "Easy, sister... easy. It was just business. You may paint me as the villain here, but look at yourselves! Drinking boiler grease at two in the morning the week before the entrance examinations! You're squandering a gift thousands would kill for! I failed my candidacy run seasons ago. Those two lads from Septal—Nickel and Tus—they just wanted a chance. I convinced them that if we took your registration receipt, they could slip into the Loom hall under your name!" <!-- L0170 --> <!-- L0171 --> <!-- L0173 --> <!-- L0174 -->

Loami stepped forward into the lantern light, thumbs hooked behind the heavy canvas suspenders of his work trousers. His jaw was set like an iron vise. "And what do you think thieves deserve around here, friend?" <!-- L0175 -->

"I'm already ruined," Azer spat bitterly, trying to sneer through the dirt. "What are you going to do? Send me to the frontier? I'm already living in the gutters. What's one more beating?"

"About eighteen points of blunt force," Loami murmured. <!-- L0176 -->

Without another word, Loami stepped in, drove his right boot into the flagstones, and planted a brutal, calloused mechanic's fist directly into Azer's floating ribs. A sharp, sickening *crack* echoed off the alley walls like snapping pine. <!-- L0177 -->

Azer's breath left him in a ragged, whistling shriek. He curled into a tight ball against the damp masonry, clutching his side, coughing flecks of blood onto the stones. He didn't speak again.

Britt slowly sheathed her dagger, stepping back to let Aggie wrap a sturdy arm around her shoulders. "What about the registration receipt?" Aggie asked, glancing into the dark passage where the two bruisers had fled. <!-- L0178 -->

"The paper is worthless," Britt muttered, rubbing the sore knot at the base of her skull. "The Loom reads the blood and the resonance. It knows who we are. They stole a scrap of dead parchment." <!-- L0179 -->

Ignatius stepped up beside Loami, looking down at the groaning thief, then across at the silver pocket watch in Britt's hand. A slow, brilliant smile spread across the flame-haired youth's face.

"Val told us back at the lounge that Lucky won't trade that study guide for gold," Ignatius said, eyes alight. "He trades for stories and unique collateral." <!-- L0190 --> <!-- L0191 -->

Loami wiped his knuckles on his work rag, chuckling softly. "A silver watch taken off an alley mugger, paired with the heroic tale of how we broke a scoundrel's ribs to protect our own? Lucky will eat it up."

Beside them, Iggy slipped off the mounting block, patting the bulging pocket of his trench coat where his freshly sketched map of the city lay tucked beside his warm amber crystal. "I have the map," the earth-kin muttered, adjusting his copper goggles. "Tomorrow after classes, we mark the quadrants. We find Lucky. We get the study guide." <!-- L0192 -->

Ignatius held out a hand, sparks dancing across his knuckles. "Tomorrow after classes. We find Lucky, we master the fundamentals, and we take the Spire." <!-- L0193 -->
