## CHAPTER 1: THE DESERT TRANSIT

The air conditioning inside the transit bus had died somewhere between the outskirts of Henderson and the endless, baking flats of the Nevada desert. 

Outside the dust-streaked windows, waves of heat shimmered over cracked asphalt, warped by the relentless afternoon sun. Inside, the cabin smelled of sun-baked vinyl, stale motor oil, and overheated machinery. The suspension squealed in agony with every dip in the road, rattling the luggage racks overhead. 

Near the back, Pierre shifted uncomfortably against the cracked seat. He adjusted his spectacles, his fingers instinctively brushing the rim of his woolen beret to make sure it hadn't slipped. He pulled his canvas rucksack tighter against his chest, staring with deep suspicion at the barren, flat wasteland stretching to the horizon. America was too big, too empty, and entirely too hot.

Suspended from the low ceiling at the front of the aisle, a boxy CRT television flickered through static, droning out a tinny midday broadcast. 

"...sweltering heat and high desert winds continue to break records across the Southwest," the news anchor’s monotone voice buzzed through the speaker. "In national news, authorities confirm that the commercial flight bound for the capital has landed safely outside D.C. We turn now to our field reporter—" 

The broadcast was instantly drowned out by a deafening, metallic shriek. 

Pierre gasped as the bus lurched violently to the left. Beneath the floorboards, the rear tires lost all traction on the superheated asphalt. Rubber smoked and spun freely, screaming as the chassis fishtailed. 

Slam. 

The violent torque tossed passengers across the narrow aisle. Luggage tumbled from the overhead racks, clattering against seats and skulls. 

"Hold on!" someone shouted over the screech of twisting steel.

Slam. 

Pierre’s knuckles turned white as he clamped onto the handrail, but physics had other plans. In an instant, the crushing G-forces vanished, replaced by a stomach-dropping, sickening sensation of total weightlessness. Pierre floated out of his seat, his beret fluttering, rucksack drifting in mid-air as the world tumbled upside down.

Slam. 

Before anyone could scream, a blinding explosion of searing heat and white light detonated across the windows. The walls of the bus dissolved into radiant phosphor. The screams, the roaring engine, and the scent of burning rubber were swallowed by absolute, ringing silence.

"Wow..." Pierre breathed softly into the void. 

And then, the light consumed everything.

## CHAPTER 2: THE LIBRARY OF THE FATES

The blinding glare did not fade so much as it crystallized into cool, morning light. 

Pierre blinked against the sudden softness, pushing his spectacles up the bridge of his nose. The acrid stench of burning diesel and scorching desert rubber was completely gone, replaced by the faint, earthy scent of ancient parchment, pressed olives, and sun-warmed stone. 

He was sitting on polished marble flagstones. Surrounding him was an enormous, open-air colonnade of fluted Doric pillars rising beneath an open sky. Colossal towering cedar shelves lined the perimeter, groaning under the weight of countless leather-bound codices, clay tablets, and unfurled papyrus scrolls. 

Standing in the exact center of the rotunda, bathed in a pool of golden sunlight, were three ancient women. 

Draped in heavy, undyed linen that seemed to drift without a breeze, their wrinkled hands worked continuously over a suspended wooden frame. Silvery, shimmering threads moved through their gnarled fingers in a blur of practiced rhythm.

The three turned their milky, unblinking eyes toward the bewildered newcomers.

"Ah, you're early," the first murmured, her voice like grinding millstones.

"Oh no," the second chimed in, head tilting at an unnatural angle. "You're late."

"Come closer," whispered the third. "Come closer... This is interesting."

Pierre scrambled backward until his spine met the solid base of a marble pedestal. He clutched his rucksack to his ribs, adjusting his beret with trembling fingers. Beneath the woolen cap, his hair sat strangely—a subtle, wild irregularity that seemed almost to twitch if one stared too closely. 

He glanced cautiously to his right. A tall, broad-shouldered man in a fitted black tank top and dark jeans was slowly climbing to his feet, checking a heavy black wristwatch on a thick forearm. Pierre decided immediately that he did not like the look of this muscular stranger, and deliberately pivoted toward the man on his left. 

"So..." Pierre asked, his voice wavering with a nervous French lilt, "is this normal for buses in America to do this type of thing?" 

The man on his left was already on his feet, dusting off the elbows of a rumpled tweed jacket. He possessed salt-and-pepper hair, keen and inquisitive eyes behind wire-rimmed glasses, and an expression not of terror, but of pure, scholarly ecstasy. 

"I am Edward," the professor announced, adjusting his lapels and gazing in awe at the monumental colonnade. "Or, as you can call me, Professor Dravin. I am a professor of Greek history. So right now... I am entirely in my element. I am loving this." 

"A professor..." Pierre echoed, eyes darting from Dravin's manic grin back to the three looming crones. "So you are telling me you know what this is?"

Across the tiles, Eusacles rolled his shoulders, his jaw tight as he took in the marble columns, the ancient scrolls, and the eccentric academic already admiring the architecture. Eusacles shook his head in pure exasperation.

"I was literally on my way to Vegas just to gamble and have a good time," Eusacles muttered, staring flatly at the two nerds standing beside him. "What about any of this seems normal to you?" 

## CHAPTER 3: THE PLANAR RIFT

Before Dravin could launch into an impromptu lecture on Hellenistic masonry, the air behind the colonnade warped with a sound like tearing silk. 

A vertical fracture split the space ten paces to the party's left. Violet and obsidian sparks hissed from the tear, bleeding viscous, pitch-black liquid into the air. 

With a guttural snarl, a creature sprang through the rift. 

It landed on all fours upon the pristine marble, its goat-like cloven hooves clicking loudly. Curved, ridged horns jutted from its brow, and intricate tribal markings writhed across its dark hide. Thick, tar-like ink oozed continuously from its shoulders and legs, sizzling wherever it dripped. As the heavy droplets struck the marble floor, one puddle bubbled upward, expanding and taking shape—a gelatinous, living blob of sentient ink that pulsed with malicious hunger. 

The horned fiend locked its yellowed eyes upon the mortals, baring jagged, needle-thin teeth. 

Behind the party, the rhythmic spinning of the loom suddenly hitched. 

"This again?" one of the Fates snapped, her ancient fingers knotting silver thread in furious agitation.

"Not again!" cried the second. "What is going on?"

Rather than standing to fight, the three sisters bent over their work in frantic unison. Their hands flew in an impossible blur, weaving threads, bleached wood splinters, and scraps of woven fabric into a tight bundle. 

"This will help!" the eldest rasped, drawing back her arm. "This will help! Hold them off—just hold them off a little longer!" 

With surprising strength, she hurled the bundle across the library. It bounced across the flagstones with a dull clatter, tumbling to a halt right between the encroaching ink monsters and the frozen party. 

Pierre leaned toward Prof Edward Dravin, peering over the rim of his glasses.

"Just trying to understand..." Pierre whispered, his voice trembling. "Are you seeing what I am seeing? Are you seeing a little guy with a sword?" 

Dravin squinted at the object. "I see the horned fiend... but yes, there appears to be a miniature figure on the floor." 

Eusacles took a step forward, his brow furrowing as he looked down at the bundle. "It looks like a messed-up Pinocchio doll. Like a Captain Hook puppet with a sewing needle." 

Lying motionless on the cold stone was an unknown named doll less than a foot tall. Its torso and limbs were carved from weathered, salt-bleached driftwood, held together by frayed sailcloth canvas. Two mismatched buttons carved from polished sea shells and pink coral were stitched across its chest. Atop its head, matted strands of dried green kelp and hemp twine mimicked hair. In its right hand, it clutched a gleaming sewing needle ground down into a razor-sharp rapier; its left arm ended in a curved, wicked steel fishing hook. 

It lay completely still, staring blankly up at the vaulted ceiling.

"Does it look alive to you?" Dravin asked cautiously. "Or is it just an object?" 

The ink beasts did not wait for an answer. With a low, bubbling hiss, the ink blob surged forward across the marble floor. 

## CHAPTER 4: INCURSION AT THE COLONNADE

Adrenaline surged through Eusacles’s veins. 

As the ink blob slithered forward, an instinctive, ancient reflex sparked deep within his chest. A faint, golden radiance flared around his forearms. Before he could consciously register the phenomenon, the heavy metal band of his wristwatch snapped open. With a sharp metallic chime, the watch unwound, spiraling up his wrist and forearm, expanding into thick, interlocking iron links. By the time it reached his clenched fist, the weight coalesced into a spiked, chain-bound morningstar glowing with latent fury. 

Eusacles stared at his armed hand for a split second, a grim grin cutting across his face.

"Well, that's dope," Eusacles muttered. 

He stepped forward and brought the morningstar down in a vicious overhead arc. But the ink blob was unnervingly amorphous; sensing the blow, it squelched sideways across the polished marble. The heavy spiked head smashed into the flagstones, scattering stone chips and ringing through the colonnade. 

"Damn it!" Eusacles grunted, wrenching the weapon free. 

Behind him, Prof Edward Dravin adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses and thrust his open palm forward. He didn't know how he knew the words, but the cadence rose naturally to his lips like an old lecture. 

"Chill Touch!" Dravin commanded. 

Beneath the ink blob, the marble floor fractured with a pale, frost-bitten glow. A spectral, skeletal hand formed from white mist erupted from the stone seams, its bony fingers clamping shut around the ink creature. The creature let out a high-pitched, wet shriek as necrotic frost burned through its viscous form, dragging it halfway down into the floor. 

"Amazing," Dravin whispered, admiring the necrotic frost radiating from his fingertips. 

## CHAPTER 5: SUNDER AND SLUDGE

Before they could celebrate, the larger horned beast bounded past Eusacles with terrifying speed. Lowering its ridged horns, it charged directly at Eusacles's ribs. 

The impact hit with the force of a battering ram. The horns crushed into Eusacles's side, lifting him off his feet and slamming him into the base of a pillar. Caustic black ink splattered across his chest, burning through his shirt like acid. 

"Ugh!" Eusacles coughed, clutching his bruised ribs as black residue sizzled against his skin. 

The wounded ink blob struggled free of the frost, sloshing forward to finish him, but its aim was wild, leaving only a puddle of acidic black sludge near Eusacles's boots. 

Near the back of the chamber, Pierre took one look at the gore, the horns, and the glowing weapons, and threw his hands up in immediate surrender. 

"Hello! Hello!" Pierre called out in his rapid, lilting French accent, taking slow, deliberate steps backward toward the safety of the library stacks. "I think there must be a misunderstanding or something here! I have nothing against anyone here. I am, you know, more a lover than a fighter! Before I left, my mother told me, 'Pierre, do not be hasty, do not make harsh judgments.' So I am just going to step over here, if no one minds!" 

The horned fiend paused, turning its ridged head toward the nervous boy. Its voice hissed like boiling tar. 

"Then come with us..." it rasped, gesturing toward the swirling black rift. "We do not need to harm you. Come with us through the rift." 

Pierre stopped, blinking behind his thick lenses. "Will that... be taking me back to Las Vegas? Because I think I am now in ancient times and I am not exactly sure." 

"It will take you where you need to go..." the beast purred. 

"Yes, okay, that sounds very pleasant," Pierre said politely, nodding with profound earnestness. He took another step backward, hooking his thumbs under the straps of his rucksack. "Fates—whom I definitely do not know because I know nothing about anything—I'm just going to go this way instead." 

Without waiting for a reply, Pierre pivoted and ducked cleanly behind a colossal bookshelf, disengaging entirely from the combat. 

## CHAPTER 6: THE DRIFTWOOD DUELIST AWAKENS

In the center of the marble floor, the limp, wooden bundle suddenly drew a sharp, violent breath. 

Gasp. 

The unknown named doll snapped bolt upright. Its shell-and-coral eyes clicked open, catching the ambient light. With uncanny agility, the little figure drew its sewing-needle rapier from its hip, spun the steel in a glittering arc, and let out a miniature, bloodcurdling battle cry. 

It charged straight across the flagstones, its little wooden joints clicking like castanets as it sprinted full tilt toward the injured ink blob, aiming its needle squarely for where an eye ought to be. 

The charge was valiant, but the ink blob splattered flat against the stone at the last fraction of a second. The needle struck marble with a shower of sparks, skittering off harmlessly. The doll tumbled forward into a roll, popping back to its feet with seaweed hair whipping across its canvas brow. 

"Toll the dead!" Dravin’s voice echoed through the colonnade. 

The professor pulled a tarnished silver bell from his tweed pocket and shook it vigorously toward the ink beasts. But whatever celestial chime was meant to resonate remained muffled—a stray piece of pocket lint was lodged deep inside the bell’s clapper, producing only a dull, pathetic thud. 

Eusacles rolled his eyes from where he leaned against the pillar. "Oh, god no..." 

Growling through the pain of his ribs, Eusacles raised his chain morningstar once more and swung at the recovering ink blob. The weapon whistled through the air, but the slippery ooze dodged under the blow once again. 

"Damn it! We are out here on the struggle bus!" Eusacles cursed. 

## CHAPTER 7: IN THE SHADOWS OF THE STACKS

Seeing the doll dancing across the tiles, the larger horned beast pivoted. It ignored the battered mortal and lunged directly for the wooden creation, sensing the divine, meddling essence of the Fates woven into its sailcloth seams. Its claws raked across the doll’s wooden shoulder, tearing splinters and sending it tumbling across the floor. 

Yet the little driftwood figure held together, its salt-stained wood flexing against the blow. 

Sensing that the tide had turned against it, the wounded ink blob turned and began slithering rapidly back toward the safety of the dark rift. 

"Oh no you don't!" Eusacles bellowed. 

As the blob darted past, Eusacles pivoted on his heel and brought his morningstar down with pinpoint precision. The heavy spiked head pulverized the creature against the marble flagstones, detonating it into a harmless spray of drying, inert black soot. 

"Love that," Eusacles breathed, wiping a streak of sweat from his forehead. 

Meanwhile, behind the towering shelves of the library, Pierre was entirely absorbed in his own mission. He walked swiftly along the endless rows of cedar shelves, keeping his footsteps silent against the stone. Around him lay thousands of ancient papyri, clay tablets, and scrolls in various states of decay. 

Pierre muttered under his breath, his voice barely a whisper: "She sent me here to find out who my parents are... If anyone would have connections between me and another, it would be the Fates." 

With the three crones focused entirely on weaving over their loom and the monsters occupied in the center of the hall, Pierre closed his eyes. He reached out with his left hand, letting his fingertips glide along the edges of the ancient parchment. 

A faint hum of magic—a gentle resonance of ancient knowledge—tingled against his skin. His eyes snapped open. He pulled three scrolls from the shelf, hastily stuffing them into his canvas rucksack, and darted silently to the next stack. 

## CHAPTER 8: TENDON SEVER

Back on the battlefield, the unknown named doll scrambled to its feet, shaking seaweed strands from its button eyes. 

It looked at the looming horned beast, then down at its own sharp fishing-hook hand. Without a trace of hesitation or self-preservation, the doll sprinted straight between the demon's cloven hooves. With a fierce grunt, it drove the steel fishing hook directly into the beast’s left Achilles tendon and yanked with all its tiny might. 

Rrrrip. 

A spray of boiling black ichor erupted from the severed tendon. The horned beast threw its head back in an agonizing screech, its rear leg giving out as it stumbled hard onto one knee. 

"Death by tendon!" the doll seemed to crow in silent triumph. 

Behind the wounded demon, the dark rift churned violently. A colossal silhouette—far larger, with towering ram-like horns and massive clawed hands—grasped the edges of the spatial tear, pulling the fabric of reality wider with a terrifying roar. 

"There's more!" the Fates shrieked in unison from their loom. "Hold them off! Just one more turn! It's almost ready!" 

Prof Edward Dravin thrust his arm toward the giant silhouette holding the portal. "Cause fear!" he cried, seeking to shatter the titan’s resolve with a wave of magical dread. 

The giant beast bared its fangs, shaking off the enchantment with a guttural, mocking laugh. It maintained its iron grip on the rift, forcing the gateway open. 

Eusacles saw the portal widening and decided enough was enough. "All I wanted to do was play some roulette," he snarled. 

Stepping forward, Eusacles wound up a desperate Spartan kick aimed squarely at the wounded demon's chest, intending to launch it backward through the portal. But as he kicked, the demon's flailing horn whipped around in a brutal counter-arc. 

The jagged bone caught Eusacles across the jaw with sickening force. 

## CHAPTER 9: THE LEGEND STRIKE

Eusacles's vision flashed white. His legs buckled, his morningstar slipping from his limp fingers as he collapsed unconscious onto the marble tiles. 

With a raspy hiss, the wounded demon reached down with clawed hands, wrapping its talons around Eusacles's collar, and began dragging the limp gambler toward the swirling oblivion of the rift. 

Across the library, Pierre dashed behind the last bookshelf. His fingers caught the leather spine of a thick codex written in ancient Greek. His eyes widened behind his glasses as he translated the title: The Guide to Gorgons. 

"This is just like the one I used to read growing up," Pierre whispered, clutching it to his chest as he felt an overwhelming, familiar kinship to the text. 

In the center of the hall, the demon was dragging Eusacles within three paces of the planar void. Dravin was out of range. Pierre was clutching his book among the stacks. 

The unknown named doll looked at Eusacles, then at the beast's remaining intact leg. An unmistakable spark of mythical destiny—the pure, heroic momentum of Legend—ignited within the doll’s sailcloth core. 

With blinding speed, the doll leaped through the air. The sewing-needle rapier gleamed with silver fire as the doll drove the point straight through the beast’s right ankle tendon, pinning it to the marble floor with pinpoint force. 

The demon let out a final, bubbling shriek. Its legs gave out entirely. It dropped Eusacles onto the cold stone and collapsed, its body dissolving into smoking puddles of black ash that were instantly sucked backward into the vacuum of the closing rift. 

"We're done!" the Three Fates cried out together. "The weave is ready!" 

From their towering loom, the three crones swept a colossal, shimmering tapestry into the air. The mythical cloth billowed outward like an enormous net, spiraling through the colonnade and swallowing the entire hall in dazzling light. The library vanished. Reality spun into darkness. 

## CHAPTER 10: THE AWAKENING ON THE DARK HIGHWAY

Cool, damp night air rushed into their lungs. 

The dazzling light collapsed into velvety darkness. Pierre tumbled softly into a grassy ditch, his rucksack safely clutched in his arms. Overhead, thousands of stars glittered through the canopy of dense pine trees. The steady, rhythmic chorus of crickets and cicadas filled the humid evening silence. 

Beside him in the ditch lay Prof Edward Dravin, the unconscious form of Eusacles, and the unknown named doll. 

Dravin immediately adjusted his spectacles and whispered an arcane invocation: "Eyes of Night." 

A soft, amber warmth settled over the party’s vision, piercing the pitch-black darkness to reveal the contours of the roadside. Beside them, an old, two-lane asphalt highway stretched into the mist, lined on one side by a sagging white wooden fence with crossbeam slats. 

Dravin knelt beside Eusacles, placing two fingers against the gambler's neck. He whispered the words to Spare the Dying. 

A soothing pulse of silver magic washed over Eusacles’s bruised body. Eusacles groaned loudly, his eyelids fluttering open as he clutched his aching head. 

"Oh, god damn it..." Eusacles groaned, rubbing his jaw. "I am so sick of the teleportation. Why do I feel hungover?" 

He blinked up at the professor and the French boy peering down at him. "Wait... how do you all know my name?" 

"You were muttering it in your sleep," Pierre answered earnestly. 

Eusacles struggled to a sitting position, dusting damp grass off his tank top. A few yards away, Dravin was examining a rusty green road sign half-buried in the overgrown weeds. 

"The sign says Charleston, West Virginia — 60 Miles," Dravin announced, pushing his glasses up his nose. 

Pierre tilted his head. "Can you remind me... is a mile one kilometer or two? Because that sounds very far." 

"About two hundred kilometers," Dravin answered with academic precision. 

"Oh," Pierre muttered, blinking. "Like Versailles to Brittany." 

"Two hundred kilometers..." Eusacles repeated, his voice climbing in disbelief. "That is so far from Vegas! Not even a little bit close! All I wanted to do was play roulette!" 

## CHAPTER 11: THE ROAD TO THE MARIGOLD

From down near Eusacles's boots, a tiny wooden hand brandished a needle rapier with an impatient swish. 

"I'm four feet below you, mate!" a sharp, raspy Cockney voice piped up from the grass. "Somebody's got to pick me up if you're trying to talk to me!" 

Eusacles frowned, leaning down to inspect the tiny speaker. The moment his face drew near, the cold, sharp point of the sewing needle gently pressed against the skin of his cheek. 

"Yeah, me," the doll muttered, its shell buttons clicking as it stood tall. "I know things. I do. I know the dark." 

Before Eusacles could recoil, the doll suddenly went limp, toppling sideways into the clover. 

Dravin immediately leaned down, tapping the wooden chest with gentle medical sternum rubs. "Come on back now, buddy..." 

With a sharp click, the doll sprang back to life, immediately swinging its tiny needle wildly at the tall stalks of crabgrass like a miniature lawnmower. 

"You're half-bloods, right?" Eusacles asked, staring between Dravin and Pierre. "Like, you're half-bloods too?" 

"On my mother's side," Dravin replied thoughtfully, "her great-grandmother was Greek. That is why I study Greek mythology." 

"Have you ever had your class come to life before?" Pierre asked. 

"I like to think every time I teach, the class comes to life," Dravin smiled warmly. 

A distant sound cut through the crickets—a faint, echoing chuckle carried on the night breeze down the fence line. Pierre and Eusacles both turned their heads toward the sound. 

"Did you hear that?" Eusacles whispered. 

Pierre knelt and gently offered his open hand to the swinging driftwood doll. "Would you like a ride?" 

The doll stopped swiping at the grass and looked up at Pierre with its coral eyes. "A ride would be grand. Thanks, mate." 

## CHAPTER 12: WELCOME TO THE MARGIN

Pierre carefully placed the doll on his shoulder, letting its driftwood legs settle against his rucksack strap. As they followed the road down the fence line, Pierre leaned his head slightly toward his tiny passenger. 

"I heard them say a word back there... 'half-blood,'" Pierre murmured in his quiet cadence. "Do you know this word?" 

"Half-blood? Yeah, I think I've heard of that before," the doll replied thoughtfully, adjusting its needle rapier. "Last time I knew where I was, me mates and me were on a ship, sailing through dark, scary waters like this. And now I'm a puppet." 

"They say Mount Olympus moved to America, you know," Pierre mused. "Maybe America is more magical." 

"A ride would be grand. Thanks, mate." 

Ahead of them, the wooden fence ended at a decrepit wooden archway leading toward a clearing in the woods. Hanging above the gate was a weathered wooden sign with several letters rotted away. What had once read *The Marigold Inn* now clearly displayed only *The Margin*. 

Through the broken gate, a crackling campfire illuminated an older, sun-weathered man and a young girl resting near the flames. As the party approached, the man turned, looking up with an easy smirk. 

"Huh? No way," the man said, raising a hand in greeting. "What's up? Welcome to the Margin. What edit killed you?" 

Overhead, the creaking sign swung softly in the mountain breeze, and the campfire cast long, flickering shadows into the night.
